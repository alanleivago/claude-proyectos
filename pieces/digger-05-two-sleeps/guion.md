# Digger 05: Humans used to sleep twice (~200 words, ~88 s)

If you wake up at 3 a.m.… you're not broken. You're doing what humans did for centuries.

Before electric light, nights were long and dark. People went to bed a couple of hours after sunset… and slept until around midnight. This is a first sleep.

Then they woke up, all by themselves, for an hour or so. And then they slept again until morning. This is a second sleep.

Operator Nina asks: "So what did they do at midnight?" Nina… they prayed, talked, checked the fire… and some visited the neighbors.

Historian Roger Ekirch found this in hundreds of old diaries and letters. Even Chaucer mentions a "first sleep", over 600 years ago.

Then street lamps and gas lights made nights brighter. People stayed up later… and the two sleeps got squeezed into one.

In 1992, scientist Thomas Wehr gave volunteers 14 hours of darkness every night. After a few weeks, many started waking up in the middle of the night… then sleeping again.

So if you're awake at 3 a.m., your body might just remember the old schedule.

Humans had a free hour every night… and traded it for a light bulb?

The full bedroom expedition is on my channel. Humans… understood. Logging off.
