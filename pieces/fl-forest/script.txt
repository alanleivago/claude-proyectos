There's a library in Norway full of brand new books by some of the most famous writers alive… and nobody's allowed to read them. Not you. Not the librarians. And the writers aren't allowed to show anyone either. You walk into a tiny wooden room, and there they are: sealed manuscripts in glass drawers, each one with a title and a name. You lean in. You can read the title. That's it. So you ask the guard when they open. He says, "In twenty-one fourteen." You do the math. You'd be… well, gone. "Don't worry," he says. "So will I." And it gets weirder. In a forest outside the city, someone planted a thousand trees just for these books. You see… it's an art project called the Future Library. Every year for a hundred years, one writer hands in one book that stays locked away until twenty-one fourteen. Then those thousand trees, fully grown by then, get cut down to make the paper the books will finally be printed on. So every writer is writing for readers who haven't even been born yet. No reviews. No fan mail. They'll never know if anyone liked it. They just write it, seal it, and walk away. Which means somewhere in that forest, right now, a tree is slowly growing into a book… that nobody alive today will ever get to read.
