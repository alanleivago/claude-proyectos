# saturno — What if Saturn crashed into Earth?

## Spec
- 9:16, 1080×1920, 24 fps, 70 s. English, paraphrased + new texts (user rule). Style **pov3d** (three.js r149), new world.
- Adapted from `Ref lunar animation/What if the moon crashed into earth.mp4` (and pieces/luna) with Saturn instead of the Moon: Saturn has 95 Earth masses, so Earth falls toward it; its icy rings arrive first as a rain of meteors; Saturn's tides tear Earth apart inside its Roche limit; we fall into its clouds.
- Format **F5, fixed place**: a handheld float ~36 m above the rooftops of a wooden fjord town at dusk (Falu-red, ochre, white houses; a white church; a quay with crates, barrels, a crane, a red shed, moored boats and a ferry; lamp posts with light pools; a red-and-white lighthouse on a rocky point with a sweeping beam; villages and pines along both shores; snowy rock walls; waterfalls).
- Saturn: an oblate banded sphere (lit, limb-darkened, the rings' shadow) and an annulus (C ring to F ring with the Cassini Division and Encke gap), growing from a golden star to filling the sky.

## Claims + sources
- Saturn: 95.16 Earth masses; mean density 687 kg/m³, less than water — NASA Saturn Fact Sheet; Sky at Night Magazine.
- Rings 99.9% water ice, extending to ~282,000 km from Saturn, ~10 m thick in most places — JPL (PIA14943); Bad Astronomy.
- Closest distance ~1.2 billion km (~746 million miles) at opposition — Love the Night Sky; Astronomy.com.
- Calculated: Earth's fluid Roche limit around Saturn ≈ 2.44 × 58,232 km × (0.687/5.51)^(1/3) ≈ 71,000 km (not on screen as a number).
- Illustrative: the tide, the impact count, Saturn's size on screen, the time scale.
