You're late, you grab one carton of milk, and you sprint to the checkout. There's just one person ahead of you. An old lady… with a single banana. Easy. Then the cashier picks up the banana… and asks how her week was. And she tells her. All of it. The grandson. The new knee. The neighbor's cat that keeps stealing her slippers. Five minutes go by. Then ten. You look up at the sign above the lane, praying it says "Closed." It says "Chat Checkout." So you try the next lane, and the guy behind you laughs. "Bro, that lane's for people who aren't in a hurry. Clearly not you." But here's the twist. That lane isn't broken, and the cashier isn't lazy. It's slow on purpose. You see, in the Netherlands, a big supermarket chain noticed that for a lot of older customers, the chat at the checkout was the only real conversation they'd have all day. And loneliness there hits more than a million people. So instead of making checkouts faster, they built one that's allowed to be slow. Cashiers who love to talk, no rush, no line of people tapping their feet. It worked so well they planned about two hundred more. So next time the line won't move, maybe don't sigh. Someone might be having the best conversation of their week… over a single banana.
