# AVA: NIGHT 1 — The Police Station (versión larga, 16:9, ~8-9 min)

Título YouTube: **Zombie Outbreak: 1 Night Locked Inside a Police Station**
Estructura (fuentes/zombie-nicho/largo/ANALISIS.md): lista de problemas con un dato real cada uno, guardado a máquina como corte de capítulo,
puerta de carga entre salas, pantalla ITEM cada ~90 s, contador de zombis en el cristal, misterio de la radio sembrado al principio y
pagado al final, compañero (perro K-9) a mitad, final en espejo con el menú YES/NO. ~1250 palabras ≈ 8:40 a 145 ppm.

cristal templado se rompe entero en trocitos; perro oye ~4 veces más lejos.

## 0:00 — Gancho
Zombies took the city at nine… and you're locked inside the old police station. Sounds like the safest place in town… it isn't. By sunrise you'll have one dog, fifteen bullets… and a very good reason not to open that door.

## 9:02 PM — How you got here
Here's how it happened. You ran out of gas two blocks away… and the only building with its lights still on was this one. The front doors were open, nobody was at the desk, and the coffee was still warm.
So you did what anyone would do… you locked the gate behind you. Click. You check the first floor. Two doors are locked, one is a closet, and one mug on a desk says World's Okayest Cop.
And that's when the radio on the front desk started talking.

## 9:15 PM — The radio
[RADIO, voz del policía por radio + tos] Emergency broadcast. Help arrives in seven days. Lights off. Gate locked. Open for no one.
Seven days. Okay. You can do seven days. But here's the weird part… the message ends, and twenty minutes later… it plays again, with the same words, the same pause and the same cough at the end.
Remember that cough.

## 9:40 PM — Problem one: the glass
First problem… the front doors are glass. It's tempered glass, so it takes a lot to crack it… but once it goes, the whole thing comes down at once, in thousands of little pieces, with no warning.
So, rule one… you never, ever go near the lobby. You push the benches against the inner doors, you kill every light you can see from the street… and you count.
There are three of them outside, just… standing there.

## 10:15 PM — Problem two: the armory
Good news… you found the armory. Bad news… someone found it first. You check every locker anyway. Empty, empty, and one with half a sandwich in it.
One pistol and fifteen bullets, and that's it. Which is fine, because a nine-millimeter shot is about a hundred and sixty decibels… that's louder than a jet taking off right next to you.
Every bullet you fire is a dinner bell for the whole block. So, rule two… the gun is the last thing you use.

## 10:50 PM — Problem three: water
But bullets aren't what gets you first… water is. You need about one gallon a day, just to drink and stay clean. That's FEMA's number. The water cooler has five gallons, and the four toilet tanks… hold about six more, as long as nobody flushes.
And then there's the one everybody forgets… the water heater in the basement. Forty gallons, just sitting there. That's fifty-one gallons, so fifty-one days, and help comes in seven.
For the first time tonight… you smile.

## 11:05 PM — Food
The break room has two vending machines, and their glass isn't tempered… one chair and a towel over the glass, and you're in. Twenty candy bars and nine bags of chips. A candy bar is about two hundred and fifty calories, so that's roughly two and a half days of food.
And that's why the water came first. There's an old survival rule of threes… about three days without water, and about three weeks without food.
The coffee maker stays off because of rule three, and honestly that hurts more than the zombies.

## 11:30 PM — The kennel
Then you hear scratching behind the door marked K-9. Every movie says don't open it. You open it. And there she is… a German shepherd, half asleep, wagging her tail like she's been waiting all night.
Her tag says Ribbon. She's got a K-9 vest, a chewed-up tennis ball, and zero interest in you until you open a candy bar. And Ribbon is better than any alarm you could build… a dog can hear some sounds about four times farther away than you can.
So now, when she growls… you listen.

## 12:00 AM — The phones
At midnight you try the phones. The desk line is dead, and your cell has one bar… and every call you make just rings busy, because the whole city is trying to call at the same time.
So you text instead. The FCC says a text can get through when a call can't, because it uses way less of the network. You text your mom one word. Safe. It says delivered, and for a minute, that's enough.

## 12:20 AM — Problem four: power
Around midnight, the backup power dies. There's a generator in the basement… and that thing kills more people than zombies do. Never run a generator inside, not in the basement or the garage, and not even with the door open. Carbon monoxide has no smell, no color… you just get sleepy, and you don't wake up.
So the generator stays off, which is one more reason for rule three… lights off, all of them. Ribbon doesn't mind, she just sleeps on your boots.

## 1:10 AM — The cells
The holding cells are down the back hall. You sweep the flashlight across them… and cell three isn't empty. A man in an orange jumpsuit with gray skin, and he heard you long before you saw him.
If you walk up to those bars to get a better look… that's the end of your night. Those doors are solid steel, and sound moves through steel more than fifteen times faster than through air, so when he hits the bars, the whole building rings.
So, rule five… nobody goes down the back hall. At the end of that hall there's one more door, with a clover carved on the lock, and it won't open.

## 1:35 AM — The survivor
On the way back, someone whispers through the records room door.
[MARLA, tras la puerta] Don't come in. Please.
Her name's Marla. She works in records, and she's been hiding in there since nine.
[MARLA, tras la puerta] There were six of them on shift tonight. I watched every one of them go out that door.
She won't open the door, and you can't blame her. So you trade, the old way. Two candy bars under the door… and a folded sheet of paper slides back out.
Then she says one more thing.
[MARLA, tras la puerta] Whatever you do, don't trust that radio.
When you come back twenty minutes later, the door is open and Marla is gone.

## 1:55 AM — The clover key
Her paper is a note from the night sergeant. He set the evidence locker to match the broadcast… days, rules, days. You already know this one. Seven. Three. Seven.
Click. Inside there's a little brass key, shaped like a clover.

## 2:10 AM — Shifts
By two, you've been up for almost twenty hours. Stay awake for twenty-four, and the CDC says you're about as impaired as someone with a blood alcohol of point one oh, which is over the legal limit.
So you sleep in shifts. Ribbon takes the first one… she lies across the doorway and doesn't close her eyes once. You get two hours, and it feels like ten minutes.
At two forty, Ribbon wakes you up with her nose in your ear.

## 2:47 AM — The save room
You find a little room with no windows, one door, and a typewriter. If you've ever played a certain kind of game… you know exactly what this is.
Before you save, you check the map. Two safe rooms, the front doors you can't use, and a back gate that's chained shut… which leaves the roof. There's a ladder up from the third floor, and that's plan B.
You save your game. Day one… two forty-seven A.M. Police station. The radio in the hall plays again.
[RADIO, voz del policía por radio + tos] Help arrives in seven days.
Same cough.

## 3:14 AM — The knock
At three fourteen, someone knocks on the glass. Ribbon doesn't bark, she just stands up… and stares.
[COP] Police department. We're evacuating the block. Open the door.
He's got the uniform, the cap and the badge. Rule four… and you're writing this one down right now… a badge is not a password. Then you check the duty log. Every officer on shift tonight… status: lost.
Including Officer Vickers. And the zombies outside? Three became eight, and eight became twenty. They're following his knocking. By three thirty, the counter on the glass says fifty.
Then Ribbon turns away from the glass and growls at the dark hallway behind you. You cross the lobby with the flashlight off, one tile at a time… and something in that hallway is breathing. You don't stay to find out what it is.
Behind you, the knocking gets faster, like he knows exactly where you are.

## 3:40 AM — The lights
He's still knocking. Then every light in the station turns on… by itself. Oh, fudge. Somebody started the generator, and it wasn't you. You grab the clover key and run to the radio room… and there's a tape recorder plugged into the transmitter, playing on a loop.
[RADIO, voz del policía por radio + tos] Help arrives in seven days.
Nobody's coming in seven days. That message is this station calling everyone in the city… right here, to you. And the voice on the tape? The name tag on the recorder says… Vickers.

## Final
So right now you've got a dog, fifteen bullets, fifty-one gallons of water, a clover key and every light in the building on… and the only officer who knows the way out is standing at the glass, knocking.
Ribbon's growling at the hallway again, and the radio is about to play. Zombies took the city at nine… and you're still locked inside the old police station.
Do you open the door? Comment yes or no… most comments decide Day two.
