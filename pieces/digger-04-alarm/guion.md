# Digger 04: The alarm clock (227 words, ~90-100 s)

If you sleep facing a window… you have an alarm clock.

But in winter… the sun shows up late.

So people pushed a nail into a candle. When the wax melted… the nail dropped onto a metal plate. Clang. This is a candle alarm.

Then came factories. If you were late, you were fired… and alarm clocks were expensive and unreliable.

So workers paid a human. A knocker-upper walked the streets before dawn… tapping on windows with a long stick. This is a knocker-upper.

Operator Jay asks: "Who woke up the knocker-upper?" Jay… most of them slept in the afternoon.

In London, Mary Smith didn't knock at all. She shot dried peas at the windows… with a pea shooter. Sixpence a week.

Rude.

Alarm clocks did exist. In 1787, Levi Hutchins built one… but it could only ring at four in the morning.

In 1876, the Seth Thomas company patented a small bedside alarm clock. Cheap clocks spread… and the knocker-uppers slowly lost their jobs. A few kept knocking until the 1970s.

In 1956, alarm clocks got a new button… to sleep a few more minutes. This is a snooze button.

Now your phone is the sun, the candle, the stick and the pea shooter.

Humans paid people to shoot peas at their windows… and then invented a button to sleep through it?

Artifact understood. Logging off.

---
**Format (new rule, ALTERNATE FORMATS):** sinaboto-style episode. It opens straight on the hook "If you [do X]… you have a [Y]", with no "Operator… I found" opener and no discovery scene. Mechayuki is still the host and acts out every step. Kept from the series: the Operator comment, the "This is a…" labels, the rising closing question and the fixed closing line.

**Closing line:** I propose keeping "Artifact understood. Logging off." It is the channel's signature, and the episode still explains an artifact. The other option, if the user wants this format to sound different: "Humans… understood. Logging off."

**Notes for voicing:**
- No "!" anywhere.
- There is no "Operator…" opener, so no spliced clip is needed.
- Two questions must rise:
  - Jay's line is a wh-question, so expect a PSOLA ramp on "knocker-upper?".
  - The closing "…sleep through it?" must rise too.
- "Clang." and "Rude." are reaction words: give each a pause before and a reaction shot.

**Timing:** 227 words; at the ep 02/03 megagirl pace (0.436 s/word) that is ~99 s of voice, plus a 2.6 s outro. The Operator comment runs from word 84 (37 %) to word 99 (44 %).

## Fact check (web, 2026-10-29)
| line | verdict | sources |
|---|---|---|
| Sleeping facing a (east) window: the sunrise wakes you; in winter the sun rises late | illustrative setup (true in general: winter sunrise is later at high latitudes) | — |
| A nail pushed into a candle drops onto a metal plate when the wax melts, and the noise wakes you | correct as a folk method. Candle clocks with nails dropping onto metal are described in secondary sources, used in monasteries and by people at home. Origin and date are uncertain, so the script gives no date or place and says "people" | [vocal.media: candle clocks](https://vocal.media/history/waking-up-with-candle-clocks-the-ingenious-precursor-to-alarm-clocks), [Ildhafn: candle clock](https://ildhafn.lochac.sca.org/terra-incognita-candle-clock-lihua-li/), [Blantyre Project: Victorian alarm clock](https://blantyreproject.com/2024/05/victorian-alarm-clock/) |
| Factories: late workers were fired; alarm clocks were expensive and unreliable | correct. Factory owners fired workers who arrived late, and knocker-ups were hired "when alarm clocks were neither cheap nor reliable" | [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/surprising-job-waking-people-up-before-alarm-clocks), [Wikipedia: Knocker-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocker-up) |
| Workers paid a knocker-upper, who walked the streets before dawn tapping windows with a long stick | correct. A long, light stick (often bamboo) for upper windows, plus a short baton for doors; they were paid a few pence a week per client. The job began in the Industrial Revolution (Britain and Ireland) | [Wikipedia: Knocker-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocker-up), [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/surprising-job-waking-people-up-before-alarm-clocks) |
| Who woke the knocker-upper? Most slept in the afternoon | correct as a simplification. Many knocker-ups were night owls who slept during the day and got up around 4 pm | [Wikipedia: Knocker-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocker-up) |
| Mary Smith in London shot dried peas at windows with a pea shooter, for sixpence a week | correct. She was an East End knocker-up from the 1900s to the 1930s. She used a pea shooter (a rubber tube) with dried peas, and charged sixpence a week. Her daughter Molly Moore carried on into WWII | [Wikipedia: Mary Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Smith_(knocker_upper)), [Wikipedia: Knocker-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocker-up), [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/surprising-job-waking-people-up-before-alarm-clocks) |
| 1787: Levi Hutchins built an alarm clock that could only ring at 4 a.m. | correct as stated by Wikipedia (Alarm clock) and History.com: he built it in Concord, New Hampshire, for himself, and it rang only at 4 a.m. He never patented it. Some pages call the 4 a.m. detail unverified. The script says "built one", not "invented the first alarm clock", which is disputed | [Wikipedia: Alarm clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alarm_clock), [History.com](https://www.history.com/news/who-invented-alarm-clock), [Wikipedia: Levi Hutchins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Hutchins) |
| 1876: the Seth Thomas company patented a small bedside alarm clock; cheap clocks spread | correct. Patent of Oct 24, 1876 (Seth E. Thomas). Strictly, the patent covered the case of a small back-winding alarm clock. Small alarm clocks became popular from the late 1870s and were mass-produced by the 1900s | [History.com](https://www.history.com/news/who-invented-alarm-clock), [clockhistory.com](https://clockhistory.com/alarmClockHistory/) |
| Knocker-uppers slowly lost their jobs; a few kept knocking until the 1970s | correct. The job mostly died out by the 1940s-50s, but it lasted in pockets of industrial England until the early 1970s | [Wikipedia: Knocker-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocker-up) |
| 1956: alarm clocks got a snooze button | correct. The GE-Telechron "Snooz-Alarm" (1956) was the first alarm clock with a snooze button. The script avoids "nine minutes", because sources disagree on the first models' delay (some measured 15-17 min) | [History.com](https://www.history.com/news/who-invented-alarm-clock), [Wikipedia: Alarm clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alarm_clock), [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1845969/why-alarm-clock-snooze-nine-minutes/) |
