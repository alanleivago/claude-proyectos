## What they do

- **They change sets every 2–4 s, and usually on every sentence.** A 30 s short goes through 6–10 different places. They almost never stay in one room for more than two lines.
- **Macro and inside-the-body views.** The camera goes inside the chest: ribs, lungs, a capsule implanted next to the heart.
- **Abstract void stages.** These are flat red or starfield backgrounds with nothing else in them: two Earths in space, a suited crew on a red void, a red grid "inside the computer".
- **Top-down overhead shots.** Agents seen from straight above, a town seen from the sky with a meat chunk falling.
- **Sky shots.** A flock of vultures against clouds, one of them vomiting meat.
- **Real-world locations,** and they are simple: an airport tarmac with Air Force One, a bank lobby, a suburban porch, a lab shelf with samples, a living room, a city street, a cinema.
- **Comic set pieces and props as heroes.** A giant keyboard, a "MASSIVE" power cord being yanked out of the wall, a "DELAYED" banner across a poster, a horse standing on a lab shelf.
- **Screen and filter changes.** CCTV vignette with grain for "invisible to the cameras", and a UV-light shot for the invisible ink.
- **Close-up versus wide.** A huge photo face fills the frame, then the next shot is a tiny figure in an empty space.
- The logo card sits inside the set (on the tarmac, on the lawn), not as a separate title card.

## Rule for punkpolyshorts

Plan at least 6 distinct set types per short: a real location, a map, a cross-section or cutaway, a macro, an abstract void, and a top-down or sky shot. Use a new set for each sentence where the story allows it, and come back to the main room at most 2–3 times.
