Imagine an auction where the price only goes down… and that's what makes it terrifying. You're a flower buyer in the Netherlands, sitting in one of the biggest buildings on the planet, with your finger on a single button. On the wall, there's a giant clock. But it doesn't tell time. It tells price. A cart full of red roses rolls in, and the clock starts high. Way too high. So you wait. The pointer drops. Then it drops again. Now it's a pretty good deal. Your finger twitches. But if you wait one more second, it'll be even cheaper. So you wait. Click. Somebody else pressed it. The roses are gone, and the guy next to you leans over. "Nice strategy. Very cheap. For him." You see, this is called a Dutch auction. Instead of people shouting higher and higher, the price falls until the first person brave enough presses the button. Nobody gets a second chance. That one rule forces everyone to show how badly they really want something, in a split second. And because one click sells a whole batch, they can sell millions of flowers every single day, so fast that a rose cut in the morning can be in a shop on another continent a day or two later. So the roses you buy for a date were probably won by someone who refused to be greedy… for exactly one second.
