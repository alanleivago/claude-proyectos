# Digger 06: How people bathed before showers (170 words, ~76 s + outro)

Five hundred years ago, there were no showers… so you could go months without a full bath. And you'd smell.

First, the river. Free… but freezing.

Rome had public baths. No soap: you oiled your skin and scraped it off with a curved metal stick. This is a strigil. But the water wasn't always fresh.

Medieval towns had steamy bathhouses called stews… with a bad reputation. In 1546, Henry the Eighth shut London's down.

Doctors said hot water let disease in through the skin. So people wore a clean linen shirt to soak up the dirt… plus perfume. This is a dry wash.

Later, the tin tub, filled pot by pot… and the whole family shared the water. The story goes, the baby went last.

In 1767, William Feetham patented a shower: pump water up by hand, pull a chain… and the same dirty water falls again.

Then came pipes and water heaters. Hot water, any time. This is a shower.

Today, the average shower takes about eight minutes. Logging off.
