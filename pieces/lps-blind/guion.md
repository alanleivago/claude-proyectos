# You're Blind 40 Minutes A Day (punkpolyshorts #3)

EN, ~40 s, about 120 words. Kaneda_punk. The last line runs back into the first one, so the video loops. Twist on the hit format: the viewer tests it on the spot in a mirror. This reuses the mirror set from #1.

| # | Voice | On screen | Kaneda expression |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | You're blind for about forty minutes every day. And you've never noticed. | Kaneda at his desk; the screen cuts to black for a frame. Big "40 MIN" in 3D. | blank |
| 2 | Your eyes don't glide across things. They jump, three or four times a second. | Inside-the-head cutaway: a PS1 eyeball snapping between points, with the jump lines drawn. | neutral |
| 3 | Mid-jump, everything would smear like a shaky phone video. | A full-screen motion smear of the room. | nervous |
| 4 | So your brain just cuts the feed. | A tiny brain in a control room pulls a lever; a CRT goes black. | — |
| 5 | Then it fills the gap with whatever you saw next, and lies about the timing. | The brain pastes a photo over the gap and stamps the time. | skeptic |
| 6 | That's why the first tick of a clock sometimes lasts too long. | Kaneda glances at a wall clock; the first second hangs. | shocked |
| 7 | Want proof? Find a mirror. Look at your left eye, then your right. | Mirror set from #1; a text card "TRY IT". | stern |
| 8 | You'll never see your own eyes move. | Close-up on the reflection: the eyes jump without any motion between. | blank |
| 9 | Your friend standing behind you sees them move just fine. | A friend (a distinct person) behind him watches his eyes darting. | annoyed |
| 10 | Add up all those tiny blackouts, and you're blind for about forty minutes every day. | Many black frames stacking up into "40 MIN". Loop. | blank |

**Props built from photos (photoprop):** the wall clock, the CRT monitor, the mirror frame and the desk items.

**Facts (to verify before the voice):**
- Saccades happen about 3–4 times per second.
- Saccadic suppression: the brain blocks the visual input during each jump.
- Chronostasis (the stopped-clock illusion): after a saccade, the brain backdates the new image, so the first tick seems longer.
- You can't see your own saccades in a mirror, but someone else can.
- "About 40 minutes a day" is a widely cited estimate of how much the saccadic blackouts add up to. In line 1 it's phrased as "about", not as an exact figure.

## Fact check (2026-11-01)
- **3–4 jumps a second:** most sources say about 3 saccades per second, and some give 3–4 or "up to three". "Three or four" sits at the top of the range but is defensible, so the line is kept.
- **Saccadic suppression / masking:** this is confirmed. The brain largely ignores the blurred input during each saccade (Erdmann & Dodge, 1898). Vision isn't fully off, but "cuts the feed" is a fair simplification.
- **Chronostasis:** this is confirmed. Yarrow et al. (Nature, 2001) found that the brain backdates the saccade target to just before the eye moved, so the first clock tick looks longer. Lines 5 and 6 match this.
- **Mirror test:** this is confirmed. You never see your own eyes move in a mirror, while a bystander does (Erdmann & Dodge; Mind Hacks #17).
- **"About 40 minutes a day":** this is a back-of-envelope popular-science estimate, not a measured value. The figures run from about 30–45 min up to 90 min or two hours, depending on the assumptions. The script already says "about", so it is kept.
- **Script changes:** none.
