### Only The Crossing Guard Stops Cars 🛑

In this town, nobody crosses the road without Miss Dot.
The road runs right through the middle of town, and it never ends. The cars have no drivers, and they never, ever brake.
Except for one thing. Miss Dot's stop sign.
She's been walking kids across for eighty years. Same orange vest. Same little whistle.
Everything else crosses the hard way.
Pizza comes over by catapult.
Weddings happen on both sides of the road. The vows are shouted.
Two old twins have waved at each other across the lanes for sixty years. They've never hugged.
My name is Rudy, and I count everything. Steps, stairs, cars, my own freckles. I can't help it.
And Miss Dot has three rules. Hold hands. Eyes forward. And never, ever count the children.
That last one was hard for me. So I counted them anyway, quietly, in my head.
Because every morning, thirty-one kids line up on our side. And every morning, thirty-one kids make it to school.
Except one day a year. The first snow.
On the first snow, only thirty make it across. Every year. Nobody talks about it. The school just puts up a new photo in the hallway.
My big brother Wes always crosses last. He says somebody has to make sure nobody gets left behind.
Then one morning, I woke up and the world was white.
First snow.
Miss Dot blew her whistle. She looked down the line, and she smiled at Wes.
"You close the line today, sweetie."
Wes winked at me. "Relax, Rudy. It's just snow."
I counted. Thirty-one kids on our side.
Then I looked across the road. The teacher was holding the door open. And she'd set out thirty chairs.
So I grabbed the stop sign right out of Miss Dot's hands, and I held it up as high as I could.
The cars didn't even slow down.
They don't stop for the sign. They never did.
And that's when I saw it. Miss Dot's pocket. A stack of little name tags, one for every year. Eighty of them.
She wasn't stopping the cars.
She was feeding them.
The line was already moving. Hold hands. Eyes forward. Wes at the back.
I ran into the road. A car came screaming at me with its horn blaring.
Then somebody shoved me back onto the sidewalk.
Wes.
He took the sign, and he stood right in the middle of the road, and he held it up, and he didn't look back.
Thirty kids made it across.
Wes didn't.
The next morning, there was a new photo in the hallway.
And Miss Dot was waiting for me at the curb. She put her orange vest on my shoulders. She put the whistle in my hand.
"You're good with numbers, sweetie. The road will need someone after me."
A brand new name tag was sticking out of her pocket.
It started to snow.
Thirty kids lined up behind me.
So tell me, do I count?
