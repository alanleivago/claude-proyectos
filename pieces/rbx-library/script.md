### Never Return A Library Book Late 📚

In this town, you can't return a library book late.
If you do, at midnight the Late Fee comes up your stairs with a rubber stamp in each hand.
You hear her before you see her. Thunk. Thunk. A stamp on every step.
If she stamps you, you become a book with your face on the cover.
But she's fair. In a hundred years, she has never said no to a renewal.
So people here read with a stopwatch.
At 11:59, parents line up at the drop box in their pajamas.
Mr. Dale was one minute late in 1979. He's in a cookbook now, between two soups.
I'm the slowest reader in school. I've had the same book since second grade. I've renewed it two hundred and twelve times.
I'm also the quiet game champion of recess. Once I stayed so quiet in a fire drill, the firemen thought I was a coat.
My neighbor Nico is seven. He takes out five books a week, just for the pictures.
Then one Sunday, he fell asleep with The Big Book of Sharks under his pillow.
It was due that day.
At 11:58, I heard it.
Thunk.
Thunk.
Thunk.
Up his stairs.
In the morning, the library window had a new book. Nico was on the cover, screaming.
We returned the shark book. It slid back out, stamped RETURNS ARE FINAL.
His mom tried paying. A grey hand came out and stamped her palm. LATE.
That night, I slid down the drop box chute.
At night, the shelves go up forever, full of kids with their faces on the covers.
And somewhere up there, thunk. Thunk.
The books whispered for help, and every whisper brought the thunks.
I didn't make a sound.
Not even when a spider landed on my nose.
I found Nico on the bottom shelf and tiptoed to the front desk.
Thunk.
She was right behind me.
Three meters of cardigan, glasses on a chain, two stamps up high.
Here's the thing about library rules. Nobody knows them better than me.
I put Nico on the counter.
"I'd like to renew this, please."
She froze.
She looked at the book. She looked at me. Then she looked at my card, with two hundred and twelve stamps on it.
In a hundred years, she has never said no.
Thunk.
New due date.
And Nico popped out of the cover in his shark pajamas.
Then I renewed every kid on every shelf.
Even Mr. Dale. He smelled like soup.
Nine hours. She stamped every one, and by the end she was humming.
Now nobody here returns anything. We just renew.
On Fridays, the Late Fee reads picture books to the little kids. She does all the voices.
She even gave me a sticker that says GOOD READER.
I finally finished my book. I renewed it anyway.
And every two weeks, I walk Nico back to the desk.
She stamps his forehead, and he giggles.
He's due back in two weeks.
