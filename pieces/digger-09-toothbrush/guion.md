# Digger 09: The toothbrush (ARTIFACT 004, 166 words, ~77 s + outro)

Operator… my drone just blew the sand off another human artifact. Humans called it… a toothbrush.

You scrub your bones with this twice a day… and it used to be pig hair?

Well… teeth. Close enough.

If you chew the end of a twig until it frays… you have a toothbrush. This is a chew stick.

People used them for thousands of years. Some still do: the miswak.

Operator Leo asks: "So who made the first real brush?" Leo… China.

In 1498, Chinese brushes had stiff hairs from the back of a pig's neck… tied to bone or bamboo. This is a bristle brush.

Pig hair. In your mouth. Gross.

The story goes, in 1770, William Addis sat in prison for starting a riot. He drilled holes in a leftover bone… and tied in bristles.

But animal hair stayed wet… and grew germs.

In 1938 came nylon bristles. They dried fast… no pig required. This is a nylon toothbrush.

Humans put pig hair in their mouths… for over four hundred years?

Artifact understood. Logging off.
