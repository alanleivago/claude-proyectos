## Fact check (2026-11-11)

**Sundman, Van Poucke, Svensson Holm, Faresjö, Theodorsson, Jensen & Roth (2019),** "Long-term stress levels are synchronized in dogs and their owners", *Scientific Reports* 9:7391 (doi 10.1038/s41598-019-43851-x), Linköping University, Sweden. It covers lines 2-10:
- **Sample:** 58 dogs (33 Shetland sheepdogs, 25 border collies) and their owners, all women. The script says "fifty-eight dogs and their owners".
- **Method:** hair cortisol (a few cm of hair) from dog and owner on two occasions a few months apart, reflecting the previous summer and winter.
- **Result:** long-term cortisol was synchronised: owners with high cortisol had dogs with high cortisol (lines 4-6).
- **Activity:** dogs wore an activity collar for a week; physical activity did not explain the dogs' hair cortisol (lines 7-8).
- **Personality:** the owner's personality had a strong effect on the dog's cortisol; the dog's own personality had no major effect (lines 9-10).
- **Caveats:** correlational and only two herding breeds. The authors infer that dogs mirror their owners' stress, so line 1 says "it might be your fault", not "it is". Competition dogs were more strongly linked than pets (not used).
- **Lines 11-13** are a deadpan illustration of everyday stressors, not findings of the study.
