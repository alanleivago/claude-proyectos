# asteroide — What if an asteroid blew apart above your town?

## Spec
- 9:16, 1080×1920, 24 fps, 70 s (1680 frames). English on-screen text, paraphrased from the reference (never its exact wording). Style **pov3d** (three.js r149), new world built on agujero's helpers.
- Format **F5, fixed place**: a porch on a hillside 44 m above a timber river town at sunrise. Reference: `Ref lunar animation/ssstik.io_@lunarcheeseee_1791514161027.mp4` (80 s: lake, green hills, cabins, a wooden rail; altitude counter, flash, plume, shockwave countdown, dust, "This really happened", Tunguska card).
- Ours: a river town of ~360 log houses with carved painted window frames, a white church with a green dome, a stone bridge, a steam train on the far bank, villagers and cows, an autumn taiga of ~50,000 trees; porch posts, a carved valance and a rail as the frame.
- Device: **light now, sound later** — the flash is silent; the loudest moment is the shockwave 26 s later.

## Claims + sources
- 30 June 1908, Tunguska, Siberia; an airburst 5–10 km up, no crater (Britannica; Wikipedia "Tunguska event").
- ~80 million trees flattened over ~2,150 km² (Wikipedia; Britannica).
- Entry speed ~15 km/s ≈ 33,500 mph (Space.com / Live Science, NASA-attributed).
- Eyewitness S. Semenov at the Vanavara trading post, ~65 km (40 mi) away: "the sky split in two", heat that almost burned his shirt, thrown from his porch (Wikipedia transcription; Slate; HNN "A Century of Tunguska").
- Illustrative: the countdown runs in compressed time (real ~3 min at 40 mi, shown 3:10 → 0:00 over 26 s); the altitude curve; the burst drawn ~25 km from the camera so it sits in frame; the mushroom-shaped plume.
