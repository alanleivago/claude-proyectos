# Odyssey p1 — storyboard (31 panels, 3:45)

Piece: pieces/odyssey-p1 · contact sheet: board.jpg · frames: pNN.jpg (each at its key word). Sound not done yet (after approval).

- **P01** (0:00.0–0:02.5) — "You've heard of the Trojan Horse." — The Trojan Horse on the plain before the walls of Troy; birds cross; slow push-in.
- **P02** (0:02.5–0:08.8) — "You've never heard what it cost the man who built it: ten years, every ship, every friend." — Odysseus alone on the sand, head bowed; three ships sink one by one on 'ship', his sailors fade on 'friend'.
- **P03** (0:08.8–0:16.7) — "On the way home he met a giant with one eye, a witch who turned his men into pigs, and a monster with six heads on six long necks." — Camera travels along the coming monsters: the Cyclops walks in on 'giant', Circe rises on 'witch', Scylla's cliff rises on 'monster'; pulls back to all three.
- **P04** (0:16.7–0:20.6) — "He went down among the dead and watched them crowd around a pit of blood." — Underworld (dark): Odysseus with his sword at a pit of blood; the shades shuffle in from both sides and crowd it.
- **P05** (0:20.6–0:29.5) — "And when he finally got home, he walked into his own house dressed as a beggar, with a bow, and a hundred men inside who wanted his wife." — Hall of columns: the beggar walks in, becomes Odysseus drawing the bow on 'bow'; camera pans to three toasting suitors and Penelope.
- **P06** (0:29.5–0:37.7) — "He left Troy with twelve ships and about six hundred men, and he came home alone. The sea took some of them, and the monsters took others." — Twelve ships leave Troy; a wave swamps the left half ('sea'), Scylla rises from the sea and takes the right half ('monsters'); one ship is left.
- **P07** (0:37.7–0:44.6) — "But if you follow the story closely, you keep finding Odysseus himself at the start of every disaster." — Odysseus in armour points out the next move; his men turn to him; push-in on 'Odysseus himself', dark wash on 'disaster'.
- **P08** (0:44.6–0:48.3) — "So let's start at the beginning, with the horse." — Chapter card: the horse rolls in, 'I · THE HORSE' is lettered.
- **P09** (0:48.3–0:54.6) — "For ten years the Greeks threw themselves at the walls of Troy, and for ten years the walls held." — Siege: five hoplites charge the walls under arrows, then fall back on 'held'.
- **P10** (0:54.6–0:58.3) — "Achilles was dead by then, along with most of the men worth anything." — Achilles dead on the plain, Odysseus grieving over him; drift to other dead; pyre smoke.
- **P11** (0:58.3–1:03.1) — "The kings were tired of each other, and the ships were rotting on the beach." — Two kings quarrel (one turns his back on 'other'); pan to tilted, cracked ships on the beach.
- **P12** (1:03.1–1:08.5) — "Odysseus was the king of Ithaca, a small rocky island nobody had much reason to visit." — Tight on a tiny Odysseus on Ithaca's summit; long pull back to a small rocky island in an empty sea.
- **P13** (1:08.5–1:15.8) — "He wasn't the strongest of the Greeks, or the bravest. What he had was a mind that never stopped looking for the trick." — Ajax (big, flexes on 'strongest') beside Odysseus; Odysseus sits and thinks; a ghost horse appears over his head on 'trick'.
- **P14** (1:15.8–1:22.9) — "His trick was a horse. A huge wooden one, hollow inside, left in front of the gates as an offering to the gods." — Three men push the horse to the gates; on 'hollow' it turns see-through and shows soldiers inside; men walk away.
- **P15** (1:22.9–1:32.4) — "Then the whole Greek army burned its camp and sailed away. The Trojans came out and found the beach empty. After ten years, the enemy had simply gone." — A soldier with a torch sets the camp tents alight, the fleet sails off; pan right: the gate opens, three Trojans step out to the horse; pan back to the empty sea.
- **P16** (1:32.4–1:39.8) — "So they dragged the horse into the city, and they drank and danced around it, and eventually they went to sleep." — Inside Troy: Trojans haul the horse in by rope, turn into dancers on 'drank', then sleepers on 'sleep' as night falls.
- **P17** (1:39.8–1:46.8) — "That night a hatch opened in its belly. Odysseus climbed down a rope with his best men and opened the gates from the inside," — Night: the hatch in the belly opens, a rope drops, three men climb down and walk to the gate, which opens with firelight.
- **P18** (1:46.8–1:52.3) — "and the army, which had only been hiding behind an island, came back in the dark." — Night sea: the fleet comes out from behind the island and rows back.
- **P19** (1:52.3–1:54.9) — "By morning Troy was ash." — By morning Troy burns: fire along the walls, smoke, falling ash, red wash, a shake on 'ash'.
- **P20** (1:54.9–2:04.2) — "The Greeks had won, but they hadn't won cleanly. They burned temples, and they dragged people away from the altars where they'd gone to beg the gods for help." — A temple burns; a woman begs at the altar; a soldier drags her away on 'dragged'.
- **P21** (2:04.2–2:06.4) — "The gods saw all of it." — Athena, Zeus and Poseidon rise on a bank of clouds and look down at burning Troy.
- **P22** (2:06.4–2:17.2) — "Odysseus loaded his twelve ships with gold and slaves and wine and turned toward home. Ithaca was a few weeks' sail away. It took him ten years." — Men load sacks, wine and a captive onto a ship as Odysseus points; the ship turns for home; a route is drawn to a small Ithaca; on 'ten years' sun and moon wheel and ten strokes are cut.
- **P23** (2:17.2–2:23.4) — "The first place they stopped was a city called Ismarus, which had nothing to do with Troy at all." — Chapter card 'II · ISMARUS': a city on a hill; two ships come in.
- **P24** (2:23.4–2:29.9) — "Odysseus attacked it anyway. His men killed the men, took the women, and carried off the wine and the cattle." — The raid: Odysseus throws his spear, his men charge, two Ciconians fall; a woman kneels; a bull is led off.
- **P25** (2:29.9–2:43.0) — "It was easy and it was profitable, and when it was done Odysseus told them to get back to the ships. They didn't listen. They were drunk on the wine and on how easy it had been, so they stayed on the beach roasting cattle," — Odysseus points back to the ships; his men sit drinking by a spit-roast and ignore him.
- **P26** (2:43.0–2:48.3) — "and while they ate, the neighbours of the city came down out of the hills." — Ciconian warriors run down the hillside.
- **P27** (2:48.3–2:55.9) — "By the time the ships pushed off, seventy-two of his men were lying dead on the sand. Six from every ship." — Bodies on the sand, the ships push off; '72' then '12 × 6' are lettered.
- **P28** (2:55.9–3:02.2) — "Then a storm caught them. For nine days the wind dragged them south, off the edge of every map they knew," — Storm: the ship pitches on huge swells, rain, lightning; nine strokes are cut; it is driven off the right edge.
- **P29** (3:02.2–3:14.1) — "and on the tenth day they came to a gentle country where people lived on the fruit of a flower called the lotus. Odysseus sent three men ashore to say hello. They didn't come back." — Gentle shore of lotus plants; the ship arrives, three men walk into the flowers and are gone; Odysseus waits on deck.
- **P30** (3:14.1–3:32.2) — "When he went after them, he found them sitting with the Lotus-Eaters, smiling, eating the flower. Nobody had hurt them and nobody was holding them there. They just didn't want to go home anymore. I think that's the strangest danger in the whole story, because you can't fight it. They had forgotten home existed." — Odysseus walks in and finds his men sitting with two Lotus-Eaters, smelling the flowers; slow push-in; a faint memory of Ithaca fades on 'forgotten'.
- **P31** (3:32.2–3:45.5) — "Odysseus dragged them back to the ships in tears, tied them under the rowing benches, and made everyone else row before anybody else could taste the stuff." — Odysseus drags a weeping man (and another follows) to the ship; the ship rows away.
