# Odyssey p5 — storyboard (43 panels, 3:18.9)

Piece: pieces/odyssey-p5 · contact sheet: board.jpg · frames: pNN.jpg (each at its key word). Voice: Mad 3 (local TTS, same reference and seeds 7/21 as p3/p4, extra seeds 3/11 for l14 and l40, picked by Whisper score), 45 lines. Sound not done yet (after approval). Starts where p4 ends (Odysseus drifting on the mast and keel) on an open panel, so the strip keeps sliding right. Chapter titles 'XI · CALYPSO' and 'XII · THE PHAEACIANS' stay 1.5 s longer than in p1/p2. New Flow layers in odyssey/cut/p5 (ring via odyssey-p5/tools/ring_p5.py); Hermes, Athena, Zeus, Poseidon, the ship, the coast, the traveller Odysseus and the recap figures are reused from earlier parts. Olympus, the Phaeacian hall and the night crossing slide in as cards; sea, shore and river panels slide as one strip (a character who appears in two strip panels in a row always stands further right in the second, so he is never on screen twice).

- **P01** (0:00.0–0:06.3) — "On the tenth night the sea threw him up on the island of Calypso." — Night: ten strokes cut; a swell carries his raft in and throws it up on the sand under two trees; 'XI · CALYPSO' lettered and held (+1.5 s).
- **P02** (0:06.3–0:13.2) — "She was a nymph, a goddess, beautiful, and she lived alone in a cave hung with vines and surrounded by springs." — Calypso's cave (new set: vine-hung mouth, four springs running down in code); Calypso (new) walks out; a glint on 'beautiful'.
- **P03** (0:13.2–0:16.9) — "She saved his life, and fed him, and fell in love with him." — On the shore he lies spent (new), then sits up; she offers him the cup (new pose); a warm glow on 'love'.
- **P04** (0:16.9–0:19.1) — "And she wouldn't let him leave." — She stands between him and the sea; dark wash on 'leave'.
- **P05** (0:19.1–0:21.8) — "He was there for seven years." — The sun wheels faster and faster over the island; seven strokes cut; he sits on his rock.
- **P06** (0:21.8–0:26.5) — "She offered him everything. Stay with me, she said, and I'll make you immortal." — She holds out the cup of ambrosia; it glows and sparks on 'immortal'.
- **P07** (0:26.5–0:29.4) — "You'll never get old, and you'll never die." — Two roundels: an old beggar (old), a dead man (die), each struck out.
- **P08** (0:29.4–0:35.7) — "And every day Odysseus went down to the beach, sat on the rocks, looked out at the sea, and cried." — He walks down to the shore and sits on his rock (new weeping pose); tears fall.
- **P09** (0:35.7–0:42.2) — "All he wanted was to see the smoke rising from Ithaca one more time, and then he was happy to die." — The camera finds a small island on the horizon with a thread of smoke; dark wash on 'die'.
- **P10** (0:42.2–0:49.4) — "He turned down living forever so he could go back to a small rocky island and a wife who was getting older without him." — Roundels: the cup of forever, struck out; a rocky island with Penelope, who bows a little on 'older'.
- **P11** (0:49.4–0:52.4) — "I've always liked him most here." — Slow push in on him on his rock at sunset.
- **P12** (0:52.4–0:54.7) — "In the end the gods took pity on him." — Olympus (card): Athena, Zeus and Hermes; a roundel of him weeping.
- **P13** (0:54.7–0:58.4) — "Poseidon was away at a feast at the far end of the world." — A route runs east across the sea from tiny Ogygia to the world's edge, where Poseidon stands by the roasting spits.
- **P14** (0:58.4–1:06.9) — "And while he was gone, Athena, goddess of wisdom, who'd always had a soft spot for Odysseus because he was so clever, went to Zeus." — Olympus (card): Athena walks to Zeus; glint on 'wisdom'; the wooden horse in a roundel on 'clever'.
- **P15** (1:06.9–1:10.7) — "Zeus sent Hermes to Calypso with an order to let him go." — Hermes flies down on his winged sandals and lands facing Calypso at her cave.
- **P16** (1:10.7–1:17.2) — "Calypso was furious. You gods can't stand it, she said, when a goddess loves a mortal man." — Calypso furious (new pose), shake; Hermes stands firm.
- **P17** (1:17.2–1:19.5) — "But she did as she was told." — Hermes rises away; she bows her head.
- **P18** (1:19.5–1:24.4) — "She gave him tools, and he cut down trees and built a raft with his own hands." — He swings the axe (new), a tree falls, the logs pile up, the raft (new, with sail) stands on the shore, then launches.
- **P19** (1:24.4–1:27.3) — "On the eighteenth day at sea he saw land." — The raft sails in; 18 strokes cut; a coast rises on 'land'.
- **P20** (1:27.3–1:30.8) — "And Poseidon, coming home from his feast, saw him." — Poseidon rises out of the sea on the left; his eye glints on 'saw'.
- **P21** (1:30.8–1:36.7) — "He gathered up every cloud in the sky and stirred the sea with his trident, and the raft came apart." — The cloud bank rolls down, rain, two bolts, the sea heaves; the raft breaks in three and he is in the water.
- **P22** (1:36.7–1:39.7) — "For two days and two nights Odysseus swam." — Day, night, day, night pass over the swimmer; two strokes.
- **P23** (1:39.7–1:44.9) — "On the third day, more dead than alive, he dragged himself up the mouth of a river onto a beach." — Three strokes; he crawls (new) out of the river onto the bank.
- **P24** (1:44.9–1:49.1) — "He buried himself in a pile of dry leaves, and slept." — Under two olive trees the leaves fall and heap over him; night falls.
- **P25** (1:49.1–1:53.7) — "He woke up to the sound of girls playing ball." — By the river the ball flies between Nausicaa (new) and a maid (new); 'XII · THE PHAEACIANS' lettered and held (+1.5 s).
- **P26** (1:53.7–1:59.0) — "A princess named Nausicaa had come down to the river with her maids to wash clothes." — A maid wrings out washing (new), clothes dry on the bank.
- **P27** (1:59.0–2:05.3) — "Then a naked man, crusted with salt, stepped out of the bushes holding a leafy branch in front of himself." — The bush rustles and he steps out holding the olive branch (new); salt glints on him.
- **P28** (2:05.3–2:06.7) — "The maids ran." — Two maids (one new) run off to the right.
- **P29** (2:06.7–2:10.7) — "The princess stood her ground." / "She gave him clothes…" — Nausicaa (new pose, holding a cloak) faces him, steady; he stands clothed on 'clothes'.
- **P30** (2:10.7–2:16.1) — "…and brought him to her father, king of the Phaeacians, who were the finest sailors in the world." — The hall (card): she leads him in to King Alcinous and Queen Arete (both new); a ship in a roundel on 'sailors'.
- **P31** (2:16.1–2:21.5) — "At the feast that night a blind singer picked up his lyre and sang about the Trojan War." — Demodocus (new) sings; guests (new); Troy burning in a roundel.
- **P32** (2:21.5–2:26.2) — "He sang about the wooden horse, and about the clever man who'd thought of it." — Roundel: the horse, then Odysseus.
- **P33** (2:26.2–2:30.4) — "The stranger at the table pulled his cloak over his face and wept." — He pulls his cloak over his face (new); push in, dark wash.
- **P34** (2:30.4–2:33.7) — "The king asked him, gently, who he was." — Alcinous leans toward him.
- **P35** (2:33.7–2:39.5) — "For the first time since the Cyclops, he said his name out loud. I am Odysseus." — The Cyclops in a roundel; he rises (new speaking pose); 'ODYSSEUS' lettered.
- **P36** (2:39.5–2:44.8) — "Then he told them everything that had happened since Troy, ending with the cattle of the Sun…" — The recap: roundels of the Ciconians, the lotus-eaters, the Cyclops, Aeolus' bag, the Laestrygonians, Circe, Tiresias, the Sirens, Scylla, the cattle and Calypso pass over the hall.
- **P37** (2:44.8–2:48.2) — "…and the name of every man he'd lost along the way." — The ghosts of his crew rise in a row behind him and fade.
- **P38** (2:48.2–2:52.0) — "The Phaeacians sat listening, in silence, until midnight." — Everyone still; the fire burns low, the hall darkens.
- **P39** (2:52.0–2:56.0) — "The next evening they put him on one of their ships, loaded with gifts." — Evening shore: the Phaeacian ship with its rowers (new), the gifts (tripod, chest, jars); Nausicaa sees him off.
- **P40** (2:56.0–3:03.5) — "And he fell into a sleep so deep it was almost like death, while the ship skimmed over the water faster than a hawk." — Night crossing (card): the ship speeds up, wind streaks, a hawk falls behind.
- **P41** (3:03.5–3:07.0) — "When he woke up, he was lying on a beach he didn't recognise." — Dawn mist; he wakes among the gifts and turns around; the ship is gone.
- **P42** (3:07.0–3:08.8) — "It was Ithaca." — The rocky island, 'ITHACA' lettered.
- **P43** (3:08.8–3:18.9) — "He'd come home late, and alone, on somebody else's ship, just as the Cyclops had prayed." — Alone on the beach with the gifts, the Phaeacian ship shrinking on the sea; the Cyclops praying in a roundel. Part 6 starts with ch 13 (Athena).
