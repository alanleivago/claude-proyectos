# Odyssey p2 — storyboard (26 panels, 2:21.4)

Piece: pieces/odyssey-p2 · contact sheet: board.jpg · frames: pNN.jpg (each at its key word). Voice: Mad 3 (local TTS, same reference as p1). Sound not done yet (after approval). Winds, splashes and storm are drawn in code in the figures' brown with orange incised edges. Open panels slide as one strip; the palace, deck and harbour sets slide in as cards.

- **P01** (0:00.0–0:03.3) — "For a while it looked like their luck had turned." — Calm sea at sunrise, four ships of the fleet sail right; the tally of twelve little ships is drawn along the top.
- **P02** (0:03.3–0:08.9) — "They reached a floating island with walls of bronze, the home of Aeolus, keeper of the winds." — The floating island with its bronze-plated walls drifts in over the sea and bobs, its shadow on the water; brown wind curls circle it on 'winds'; 'IV · AEOLUS' is lettered.
- **P03** (0:08.9–0:14.4) — "Aeolus liked Odysseus. He kept him for a month, asking for story after story about Troy." — Palace hall (card slide): Aeolus seated, listening; Odysseus (traveller, cup in hand) tells stories; a little frieze between them where the wooden horse and two warriors rise on 'story'/'Troy'; moons wheel past on 'month'.
- **P04** (0:14.4–0:19.4) — "And when it was time to leave, he gave him a leather bag tied shut with a silver cord." — Aeolus walks over with the leather bag; Odysseus waits; the silver cord glints on 'cord'.
- **P05** (0:19.4–0:23.5) — "Inside it he'd trapped every wind that could blow them off course." — The bag fills the picture, pulsing; on 'trapped' a round cutaway in its belly shows the winds churning as brown spirals; two little gusts strain at the knot.
- **P06** (0:23.5–0:27.2) — "He left only the west wind free, to carry them straight home." — One gust (the west wind) streams in from the left and pushes the ship; the bag sits on deck; a dashed route is drawn to Ithaca on the horizon on 'home'.
- **P07** (0:27.2–0:36.3) — "They sailed for nine days and nine nights. Odysseus held the steering oar himself the whole time, because he didn't trust anyone else with it." — Big ship, Odysseus standing at the steering oar on the stern, two men on deck; sun and moon wheel overhead, nine strokes are cut; the camera closes in on him, a short night wash on 'the whole time'.
- **P08** (0:36.3–0:38.5) — "On the tenth day they saw Ithaca." — Tenth day: Ithaca rises out of the horizon; Odysseus at the bow shades his eyes, his men stand up; ten strokes stay at the top.
- **P09** (0:38.5–0:43.2) — "They were close enough to see the smoke of the fires and people moving on the shore." — Long push into Ithaca: houses on the slope with hearth smoke on 'smoke', tiny people walking on the shore on 'people'.
- **P10** (0:43.2–0:48.3) — "And Odysseus, after nine days without sleep, closed his eyes." — The deck (card slide; paper sky, low rail, mast and sail on the right, Ithaca small on the horizon): Odysseus at the oar sags and turns into the sleeping pose on 'closed'; the oar swings loose; a light dark wash.
- **P11** (0:48.3–0:51.1) — "His men looked at the bag and started whispering." — Deck: Odysseus asleep left; the bag in the middle; one man crouches and whispers behind his hand, two others lean in.
- **P12** (0:51.1–1:00.5) — "What did the king get from Aeolus that he's hiding from us? Gold, probably, or silver. We fought the same war he did, and we're going home empty-handed." — Imagined cups and coins appear over the bag in cream/gold outline on 'gold' and 'silver'; one man points at the sleeping king on 'king'; another shows his empty hands on 'empty-handed'.
- **P13** (1:00.5–1:02.8) — "So they untied the silver cord." — A man kneels at the bag and pulls the silver cord loose; the bag swells and two gusts start leaking out; Odysseus still asleep.
- **P14** (1:02.8–1:05.7) — "Every wind in the world came out at once." — The bag bursts: brown gusts fly out in every direction, the sail tears into strips, the men are flung back, rain, shake on 'once'; Odysseus wakes shouting.
- **P15** (1:05.7–1:12.8) — "The storm picked up the ships and blew them all the way back across the sea, right back to the island of Aeolus." — Storm: huge swells, gusts from the right, lightning; the ships are hurled back to the left; the dashed route runs back from Ithaca; the floating island slides in on the left on 'Aeolus'; the fleet tally (still 12) at the top.
- **P16** (1:12.8–1:15.8) — "Odysseus went and begged him for help a second time." — Palace (card slide): Aeolus seated facing left; Odysseus walks in and falls to his knees on 'begged'.
- **P17** (1:15.8–1:22.6) — "Aeolus took one look at him and told him to get out. A man the gods hate that much, he said, I won't lift a finger for." — Aeolus stands up and points him out on 'out'; Odysseus turns and walks away left; dark wash on 'hate', lifts after 'finger'.
- **P18** (1:22.6–1:31.0) — "They'd been close enough to see the smoke from their own homes, and now they were further away than when they'd started." — Open sea: the ship with Odysseus far left, Ithaca and its smoke small on the right; a dashed double arrow between them stretches as the ship drifts back on 'further'.
- **P19** (1:31.0–1:33.2) — "There was no wind now, so they rowed." — Flat calm, sail furled; the ship lunges forward stroke by stroke; 'V · THE LAESTRYGONIANS' is lettered.
- **P20** (1:33.2–1:41.3) — "Six days of it, until they reached a harbour with high cliffs on both sides and a narrow mouth, the water inside as calm as a pond." — The harbour: two rocky headlands drawn in code like the Cyclops mountain (irregular ridge, strata, hatch, stones, scrub) with sheer faces, the narrow mouth between, ships arriving; the water calms on 'pond'; six strokes are cut.
- **P21** (1:41.3–1:48.1) — "Eleven ships went in. Odysseus, careful for once, tied his own ship to a rock outside the entrance." — Card slide (harbour set from here on): eleven ships glide in between the cliffs one by one, '11' lettered on 'eleven'; Odysseus's ship stays outside on the left and a rope runs to a rock on 'rock'.
- **P22** (1:48.1–1:54.5) — "The land belonged to the Laestrygonians, who were giants as well, though these ones didn't bother with a cave." — Giants rise behind the cliff tops: Antiphates (crowned king, club) on the right cliff on 'Laestrygonians', three more on 'giants' (two with boulders, one with a trident).
- **P23** (1:54.5–2:01.1) — "They came to the cliffs by the thousands and threw boulders down, and the ships broke apart like eggshells." — Six giants on the cliff tops heave boulders; rocks arc down one after another, splashes leap, each ship breaks in two and sinks; shake on 'eggshells'.
- **P24** (2:01.1–2:05.3) — "Then they speared the men in the water like fish and carried them home for dinner." — A giant on a ledge at the foot of the right cliff spears the men in the water one by one (they go under); broken hulls sink; on 'home' he turns and walks off.
- **P25** (2:05.3–2:10.3) — "Odysseus cut his mooring rope with his sword and screamed at his crew to row." — Odysseus on his ship raises his sword and the mooring rope parts on 'cut'; he shouts on 'screamed' and the ship pulls away to the left on 'row'; splashes keep falling in the harbour.
- **P26** (2:10.3–2:21.4) — "They got away, but they were the only ship that did. Of the twelve ships, one was left, with maybe forty men on board." — Alone on the open sea; the fleet tally: eleven ships are struck out with brown crosses, the last one is circled on 'only'; '1 OF 12' on 'left', 'ABOUT 40 MEN' on 'forty'.
