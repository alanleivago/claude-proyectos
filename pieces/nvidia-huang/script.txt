Imagine you're Jensen Huang, and in just two weeks you go from running the company behind the AI boom to handing in your resignation. You co-founded Nvidia, and you've worn the same leather jacket on stage for years. Then, in late October, the New York Times reports that you're close to a businesswoman named Alice Huang. No relation. She helped set up Megaspeed, a Singapore company that buys billions in your chips. And the Times goes further. According to the paper, U.S. intelligence believes she was recruited by China's spy agency. China calls that a smear, and nobody has been charged. So on a Sunday in November, you email your whole company: "I was unfaithful to my wife." You don't say who. Your board opens an investigation the same day. On Monday your stock slides about five percent, and the president says the FBI is looking into it. On Wednesday the Times reports a second relationship, with an executive who reports to you. On Thursday Reuters reports another one. Your intern stares at the chart and says, "Boss, is that a graph or a ski slope?" You see, when one man is the face of a company this big, investors are buying him along with the chips. That same day, Nvidia files a notice. It's official. Thirty-three years, and the end took two weeks.
