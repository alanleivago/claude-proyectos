# nvidia-huang — script options (61 s, 9:16, EN narrator)

First short of the "famous people in cartoon3d" format. Every claim is in [facts.md](facts.md). Humanizer pass done; no "!".
Legal: allegations are always attributed (NYT, Reuters, "the president says"); China's denial and "nobody has been

is said exactly as the 8-K says it (no reason given). The fictional secondary characters (intern, neighbour, agent)

## Option A — Huang's POV (recommended) · 219 words
Hook (0–1.5 s): "Imagine you're Jensen Huang." Hero on a keynote stage in the leather jacket, a huge glowing chip rising behind him.

Imagine you're Jensen Huang. You co-founded Nvidia, the company whose chips power the AI boom, and you've worn the same leather jacket on stage for years. Then, in late October, the New York Times writes about you and a businesswoman named Alice Huang. No relation. She helped set up Megaspeed, a Singapore company that buys billions of dollars of your chips. Then the Times goes further. According to the paper, U.S. intelligence believes she was recruited by China's spy agency. China calls that a smear, and nobody has been charged. So on a Sunday in November, you email your whole company: "I was unfaithful to my wife." You don't say who. Your board opens an investigation the same day. On Monday your stock slides about five percent, and the president says the FBI is looking into it. On Wednesday the Times reports a second relationship, with an executive who reports to you. On Thursday Reuters reports another one. Your intern stares at the stock chart and says, "Boss, is that a graph or a ski slope?" You see, when one man is the face of a company this big, investors are buying him along with the chips. That Thursday, Nvidia files a short notice. You've resigned, effective immediately. Thirty-three years running the company, and the end took two weeks.

Wordless gags:
- G1 (after "unfaithful to my wife"): he presses SEND; every phone in the open-plan office buzzes at once and the whole floor slowly turns to stare at his glass office.
- G2 (after the intern's line): the intern grabs a pair of skis and slaloms down the falling stock chart and out of the window.
- G3 (closing): an empty keynote stage; the spotlight lands on the leather jacket hanging alone on the mic stand; the janitor tries it on, checks his reflection, and quietly hangs it back up.

## Option B — the investor's POV · 221 words
Hook (0–1.5 s): "Imagine you own one share of Nvidia." A single golden share certificate in the hero's hands, a rocket-shaped chart behind him.

Imagine you own one share of Nvidia. It's the company whose chips power the AI boom, and for years your share mostly went up. Then, one Sunday in November, the CEO, Jensen Huang, emails his whole staff: "I was unfaithful to my wife." He doesn't say who. But news reports point to Alice Huang, no relation, a businesswoman who helped set up Megaspeed, a Singapore company that buys billions in Nvidia chips. And according to the New York Times, U.S. intelligence believes she was recruited by China's spy agency. China calls that a smear, and nobody has been charged. On Monday your share drops about five percent. The president says the FBI is looking into it. On Wednesday the Times reports a second relationship, with an executive who reports to Huang, and after hours your share slides about seven percent more. On Thursday Reuters reports another employee. Your neighbor checks his phone over the fence and says, "Good news, it's on sale. Bad news, you already own it." You see, when one man is the face of a company this big, investors are buying him along with the chips. By Thursday, Nvidia files a notice. Huang has resigned, effective immediately, and the finance chief is running things for now. Your one share is still yours. It just weighs a little less.

Wordless gags:
- G1 (after "about five percent"): the share certificate in his hands shrinks a little, like a sweater in the dryer.
- G2 (after the neighbour's line): a giant "SALE 50% OFF" sticker slaps onto the hero's own house.
- G3 (closing): he puts the paper-thin share in his wallet; it floats out like a feather and drifts away down the street.

## Option C — spy-thriller parody · 222 words
Hook (0–2 s): "The man whose chips power the AI boom just resigned, and the story plays like a spy movie." Film-noir title card, searchlights, the hero's silhouette in the leather jacket.

The man whose chips power the AI boom just resigned, and the story plays like a spy movie. Opening scene. Jensen Huang, leather jacket, co-founder of Nvidia. Enter Alice Huang, no relation, a businesswoman who helped set up Megaspeed, a Singapore company that buys billions in Nvidia chips. In late October, the New York Times reports the two are close. Plot twist. According to the Times, U.S. intelligence believes she was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. China calls it a smear, and nobody has been charged. Act two. Huang emails his staff: "I was unfaithful to my wife." He doesn't say who. His board opens an investigation, the stock drops about five percent, and the president says the FBI is looking into it. Act three. The Times reports a second relationship, with an executive who reports to him. A day later, Reuters reports another one. An agent in sunglasses lowers his binoculars and says, "Sir, I think we're in the wrong movie. This one's a soap opera." You see, a spy movie ends with a chase or a rescue at the last second. A real company ends with paperwork. Nvidia files a short notice: Huang has resigned, effective immediately, and the finance chief takes over for now. On an empty stage, a spotlight is still waiting for a leather jacket.

Wordless gags (the "movie" frame is the staging; the facts stay attributed):
- G1 (after "Plot twist."): a clapperboard snaps shut, and the set swaps from a sunny tech campus to rain and neon.
- G2 (after the agent's line): the agent's binoculars show a TV soap opera; he sighs and changes the channel with a remote.
- G3 (closing): the spotlight sweeps the empty stage looking for the jacket, gives up, and switches itself off.
