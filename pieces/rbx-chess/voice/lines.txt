In this kingdom, you can only move like your chess piece.
Rooks are the taxis. Straight lines only. Fastest ride in the kingdom, as long as you don't need to turn.
My aunt is a white-square bishop. She married a black-square bishop. They've never held hands. They wave.
And every Sunday is Game Night. The clouds slide back like a lid, a Hand comes down, and it drops one of us in the Box.
Nothing comes back out of the Box.
My dad's a knight. He moves in an L and he can jump over things, so some mornings he lands on the baker's roof.
I'm a pawn. One square a day. Forward. I'm on row seven.
Dad never could walk me to school. Jump, land, wait for me, jump. Took us forever.
Tiny print at the bottom of the rulebook: if a pawn reaches the last row, it can become anything.
No pawn ever has. We get captured first.
This Sunday, the Hand came down over our street.
Past the bakery. Past my aunt and uncle, waving. And it stopped right over me.
Dad jumped. Two over, one up. Right in front of me.
The Hand took him instead.
I tried everything.
I asked a rook. "Straight lines only, kid. The Box is diagonal."
I asked my aunt. She can go diagonal. She can't carry a pawn.
I went all the way to the queen. She can go anywhere. She looked down at me and said, "Pawns don't get rescues."
I even asked the king. He said he'd love to help, but he's been trying to leave his kitchen since 1987.
And I can only move one square.
So I looked at row eight. Nothing but fog. No pawn has ever stepped in it.
I took one step.
The fog cleared, and the whole kingdom was staring at me. Well, everyone who could get there in time. The rooks got there first.
A voice from the sky asked, "What do you want to be?"
Everybody expected a knight. Like Dad.
"Queen."
And I walked sideways. Then backwards. Then diagonally, all the way across the board, straight to the Box.
Inside it was dark and wooden, and stacked to the ceiling with every piece ever captured. Some had been in there so long they'd lost their color. Dad was right on top.
But the walls were way too high. A queen can go anywhere, as long as nothing's in the way.
A knight can jump.
Dad grabbed me and jumped. Over the wall. Over the street. Onto the baker's roof.
The baker didn't even look up.
Up in the sky, the Hand stopped. Then it slowly went back behind the clouds.
Now I can go anywhere I want, in any direction, as far as I want.
But every morning, Dad and I still walk to school in an L. Jump. Land. Wait. Jump.
It takes us forever.
Best part of my day.
