### Never Lose A Sock 🧦

In this town, you never lose a sock.
You see, every sock that goes missing ends up with the Sock Man. And the day he has both socks of a pair, he comes up the laundry chute for the feet that go in them.
So nobody here takes their socks off. Ever.
We sleep in them. We shower in them. When a baby's born, the nurse puts the socks on before the hat.
The mailman ties his to his ankles with string. Mr. Ortega counts his out loud every morning on the porch. "Left. Right. Left. Right." The whole street can hear him.
Grandma knits her socks straight onto her feet. She hasn't seen her toes since 1971. She says she doesn't miss them.
And at school, there's a sock check at the door. If one's missing, you go home, and your mom goes through the dryer with a flashlight.
Nobody's ever seen the Sock Man. But some nights the drain in the laundry room makes a sound, like somebody counting.
I wear this hood every day. And I've never worn a matching pair of socks. Not once. Today it's a dinosaur on the left and a pizza on the right. Mom says I dress like the lost-and-found box.
My little sister Pip is four. She has yellow socks with ducks on them, and she loves them more than anything. More than me, probably.
She also takes them off everywhere. Even at dinner, which is gross.
Until one Saturday, she came home from the park with one bare foot.
The left duck sock was gone.
We looked everywhere. Under the couch. Inside the dog. Okay, near the dog.
That night, the laundry chute rattled.
Mom bought her forty new pairs. Didn't matter. He wanted the ducks.
A week later, Pip fell asleep on the couch, and the right duck sock slid off her foot. I watched it crawl across the carpet and slip into the vent.
Now he had a pair.
At midnight, the dryer turned on by itself. Wet footprints came up the stairs. Sock-shaped ones.
Then an arm came out of the laundry chute. Long and grey, made of a thousand old socks. It wrapped around Pip's ankle and pulled.
And that's when I realized something.
He only comes when he has a pair.
And a pair has to match.
So I let go of Pip.
I dove into the chute.
Down and down, into a cave of socks bigger than our school. Gym socks, baby socks, one sock that was definitely Grandpa's. And right at the top, pinned up like a trophy, two yellow ducks.
I pulled off my pizza sock.
I grabbed one duck.
Swap.
Duck. Pizza.
Not a pair.
Upstairs, the arm let go of Pip.
And down in the dark, the Sock Man just sat there holding a duck and a pizza. He looked really confused.
After that, everything changed.
Now nobody in this town wears matching socks. Grandma only knits lefts. Mr. Ortega still counts every morning. "Left. Left. Left."
And every Sunday, we drop one odd sock down the chute. He likes the pizza ones best.
So if you ever lose a sock, don't go looking for the other one.
