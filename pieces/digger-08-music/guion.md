# Digger 08: How people listened to music before Spotify (163 words, ~72 s + outro)

Three hundred years ago, there was no Spotify… so if you wanted music, you had to make it yourself. Badly.

Rich homes hired musicians to play live… but you had to pay the band.

In 1796, a Geneva watchmaker fit a song inside a tiny box. This is a music box. Same song… forever.

In 1877, Thomas Edison recited a nursery rhyme into a horn… and it played back. This is a phonograph. The tinfoil wore out after a few plays.

Then Emile Berliner's flat discs. You cranked the gramophone by hand… for about three minutes of music.

In the 1920s, radio brought music home for free… but the station picked the songs.

In 1979, the Walkman put tapes in your pocket. Until one tangled… and you fixed it with a pencil.

Now almost any song streams to your phone in seconds.

About a hundred thousand new songs are uploaded every day. Hearing one day's worth would take over half a year. Logging off.
