## Folklore check (2026-10-29)
- A woman's head on a long serpent body, wet hair, seen on the shore (often washing her hair). Edo-period paintings (Sawaki Sūshi, Toriyama Sekien) show the serpent body; narrative accounts of the body vary.
- Shimane / Iwami and northern Kyūshū: she hands a passer-by a baby; once held it grows heavy as stone and pins the victim, and the ushi-oni comes out of the sea to kill them. In an Ōda (Shimane) tale a cornered ushi-oni speaks in her voice, so some say they are one being. Kyūshū variant: iso-onna.
