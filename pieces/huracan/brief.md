# ¿Cómo se forma un huracán? — brief

**Spec:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 24 fps, 62 s, 120 BPM. Music + box captions, Spanish (km/h). Style `pixdoc` (360×640).
**Format:** F2 chronology of a process; top views and side sections alternate, joined by mosaic zooms. Adapted from the "Hurricane Winds Reach 157 MPH" reference; added: the storm beach hook, the thermometer, the inflow at the base, the globe (spin per hemisphere, no storms at the equator), the hurricane-hunter plane, the power grid lighting up, the storm surge, the cycle ending.
**Device:** a wind counter (km/h + Saffir-Simpson category colour) rises and falls; heat is always orange.

## Claims and sources
- Forms over seas warmer than ~26.5 °C; not within ~5° of the equator (Coriolis) — [Stull, Practical Meteorology](https://geo.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Meteorology_and_Climate_Science/Practical_Meteorology_(Stull)/16%3A_Tropical_Cyclones/16.02%3A_Section_3-), [RMetS](https://www.rmets.org/resource/what-is-a-hurricane)
- Category 5 above ~250 km/h (252 km/h = 157 mph) — [Belspo](https://eo.belspo.be/en/hurricane-alert)
- Heat released ≈ 6×10¹⁴ W (≈5×10¹⁹ J a day) — [NOAA AOML FAQ](https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/hrd/tcfaq/D7.html); world electricity ≈ 30,000 TWh a year ≈ 1.1×10²⁰ J, so one day ≈ half a year (the reference's comparison; generation figure: standard recent value, Ember/IEA).
- Spin: counter-clockwise in the north, clockwise in the south (standard).
