# Thursday Sky 🌙 — guion v2 (EN, ~2 min)

In this town, nobody looks up on Thursday nights.
You see, something lives in the sky. And every time it catches someone looking, it comes down a little lower.
So on Thursdays, the whole town keeps its eyes on the ground.
Every window gets boarded up at sunset. The mailman walks his route staring at his shoes. The pizza guy delivers by smell. Mr. Ortega drives to work with a cereal box over his head, and somehow he's never been late.
Grandma waters her plants with her eyes closed. She's been doing it for sixty years. She's honestly pretty good at it.
And there's a rule at school. If you hear the bell on a Thursday, you put your head on the desk and count to a thousand.
We call it the Watcher.
Nobody knows what it looks like. Nobody wants to find out.
Me? I'm ten. I wear this hood every day. And I'm the best staring-contest player in the whole school. I've never blinked first. Not once. Not even against my cat.
My little sister Pip is four. She doesn't get the rule yet. She thinks the boards are there so the house can't see out.
Until one Thursday, I woke up at midnight because the boards were creaking.
Pip was at the window. She'd pulled one board loose. Just one.
And she was looking up.
And something was looking back.
A face. Bigger than the moon. Grey, with two orange eyes.
I grabbed her and pulled her away. But it was too late. It had seen us.
The next Thursday, it was lower. Right behind the church bell.
The Thursday after that, it was over the treetops. The whole street could hear it breathing.
Dad nailed more boards. Then more. Mom stopped sleeping. The neighbors started packing their cars.
I tried everything. I wrote "GO AWAY" on our roof in chalk. It came lower.
I covered Pip's window with every blanket we had. It came lower.
On the last Thursday, it was so low the shingles rattled. And it pressed one giant eye right against our window.
And that's when I realized something.
The rule says it comes down when it catches you looking.
But it never says what happens if you don't stop looking.
So I climbed out onto the roof.
And I stared.
It stared back.
One minute.
Ten minutes.
An hour.
My eyes burned. The wind was freezing. I didn't blink.
Then it did something nobody in this town had ever seen.
It blinked.
And it went up. Past the treetops. Past the church bell. All the way up, until it was just a tiny grey dot.
After that, everything changed.
Now on Thursday nights, the whole town climbs onto their roofs. Grandma brings cookies. The pizza guy finally sees where he's going.
And we all stare at the sky. Together.
It hasn't come back.
But some Fridays, I swear I see it up there, practicing.
