Some beaches in England have a very strange tide. After a big storm, the sea spits out Lego. A woman walks along the sand, picks up something tiny and black, and it's a little plastic dragon. Then another. Then a flipper. Then a tiny life jacket. And here's the funny part. This has been happening for almost thirty years. Back in 1997, a cargo ship off the coast of Cornwall got slammed by a giant wave, and sixty-two containers fell into the ocean. One of them held almost five million Lego pieces. And of all the sets that could have gone overboard… most of these were sea themed. Scuba tanks. Spear guns. Seaweed. Octopuses. So the ocean basically got its own Lego collection. Then her neighbor walks by with his dog. "Still cleaning up after a wave that hit before your phone was even invented?" You see, plastic doesn't really go away. It floats, it sinks, it rides the currents around, and then storms push it right back to shore. Ocean scientists even tracked the pieces to learn where floating trash ends up. So now people go dragon hunting after every storm. The black dragons are pretty common… but a green dragon is so rare that finding one is the beach version of winning the lottery. All because one wave knocked over one box… that refuses to leave.
