# Your Dog Can Smell How Late You Are (zaw-dog #1)

EN, about 42 s, 129 words, deadpan narrator. Cast: the dog (brown-and-white mixed breed) and the crude low-poly owner, in cosy home sets with real depth. The last line repeats the first, so the video loops. This is the dog version of ZawPolyShorts' biggest hit ("What Your Cat Thinks You're Doing When You Leave The House?", 20M), with a twist. Their cat thinks you go hunting, which is made up. Our dog doesn't care where you go: it times you with its nose, and that part is real research.

## Research: dog versions of the ZawPolyShorts premises (2026-11-05)
Searched with yt-dlp `ytsearch` and the Shorts filter URL (`sp=EgIYAQ%253D%253D`), using a generic UA.
- **"What your dog thinks when you leave the house":** only long-form versions exist. Jaw-Dropping Facts got 765K views (9 min, 2023); Will Atherton got 28K. Small channels (DogFrame, Mecko, DogMind) got under 1K. No animated or low-poly Short version was found. A video titled "Your Dog Still Thinks You're a Hunter" (Vaultline) had 1 view.
- **"Dogs smell time":** this angle is proven. BBC "Can dogs tell the time?" got 2.3M views (3.5 min, 2014), The Dodo "Dogs Can Actually SMELL Time" got 314K, and Goldie & Frenchie "Dogs can smell time?!" got 251K (81 s, Oct 2025). The few true Shorts on it had tiny numbers (16 and 281 views), so the angle is underserved in Shorts.
- **Shower/bathroom:** "Why dogs follow you into the bathroom" got 4.7M (Facts Verse, long-form), but there is no "your dog thinks you're weak" version. It's weaker for dogs, because dogs following you to the bathroom is a known cliché.
- **Trust / new dogs hate each other:** these are only training videos (Beckman, Zak George, McCann), in a how-to tone. They don't fit a deadpan Short.
- **Pick:** "leave the house" gives us the 20M title shape plus the smell-clock twist, and it's the one place where long-form views are big and Shorts are empty.

## Fact check (2026-11-05)
- **Scent fading as a clock (lines 3–5):** this is Alexandra Horowitz's hypothesis (Barnard Dog Cognition Lab, *Being a Dog*, 2016): "Dogs smell time." She calls it an untested hypothesis, which is why line 4 says "thinks".
- **BBC dog (lines 6–8):** in *Inside the Animal Mind* (BBC, 2014, Chris Packham), the dog Jazz habitually got on the sofa before his owner came home. After the owner's worn T-shirts were wafted around the room during the day, he stopped doing it. This was an informal TV test, not a study, so the script says "on the BBC". The "20 minutes before" figure appears only in secondary press, so it's left out.
- **Swedish study (lines 9–10):** Rehn & Keeling (2011), *Applied Animal Behaviour Science* 129. Twelve dogs were left for 0.5, 2 and 4 h. After the longer absences the greeting was more intense (more tail wagging, more contact, more activity). Two hours against four showed no clear difference, so the script says "the longer waits" and not "the longer you're gone, the bigger". The authors note that this doesn't prove the dogs were aware of the time.
- **Dogs mostly rest when alone:** the same study found this. It's not in the voice, but it's usable in shot 9.
- **Left out on purpose:** the "300 million vs 6 million receptors" numbers, because sources disagree (200M–1B). Also left out: Hepper & Wells (2005), dogs tell trail direction from 5 footsteps. It's verified, but there was no room for it.
- **Script changes:** none needed after the check.
