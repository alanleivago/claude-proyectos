## Fact check (2026-11-16): the script checks out, no changes

**Study:** Schünemann, Keller, Rakoczy, Behne & Bräuer (2021), "Dogs distinguish human intentional and unintentional action", *Scientific Reports* 11:14967. The lead author is at the University of Göttingen. The dogs came from the Max Planck Institute for the Science of Human History in Jena. 51 pet dogs were analysed.

**Setup (L2):** an experimenter passed food through a 15 cm gap between two walls, each a wooden frame holding transparent plastic. "Glass wall" is the plain-speech version of a transparent plastic screen.

**The three conditions:**
- **Clumsy (L3):** the food was "dropped accidentally" ("oops!") and rolled to her side.
- **Unwilling (L4):** she "intentionally pulled the reward away in a fast motion" ("ha-ha!").
- **Blocked:** the gap was closed. It is not in the script.

**Lines 5-6:**
- In every condition the dogs could walk around the screen to get the food. The latency before going around was significantly higher in the unwilling (teased) condition than in clumsy or blocked (p < .001). On average, 43 % of a dog's total waiting time was in the unwilling condition, against 32 % in clumsy and 25 % in blocked.
- **"Waited way longer" is a mild stretch:** the difference is clear and significant, but it is a relative measure.
- **Tail:** "ceasing tail movement" happened 18 times across 15 dogs, and 78 % of those times (14) were in the unwilling condition. **"Stopped wagging their tails"** is right about the direction of the effect, but it was a minority behaviour, not every dog.
- **Not in the script:** dogs also sat or lay down more often when teased (65 % of the 17 occurrences).

**L7 is a deadpan extrapolation.** The study did not test fake throws, but the authors frame the result as dogs recognising "intention-in-action".
