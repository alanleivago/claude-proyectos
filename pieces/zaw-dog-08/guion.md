# Why New Dogs Instantly Hate Each Other (zaw-dog #8)

EN, ~17 s, ~50 words, deadpan narrator, Zaw 5-beat structure (no loop), ~13 cuts of 1.0-1.4 s. Dog version of Zaw's "Why New Cats Instantly Hate Each Other" (10M, checked 2026-11-12). PawPoly has no video with this title or topic. Not a repeat of #6 (owner stress) or #7 (lying): the twist here is the leash forcing a rude greeting.

**Fact base:** canine greeting etiquette (standard ethology): a curved approach + side / rear sniff is polite; a straight, staring head-on approach reads as a challenge; a short tight leash forces the head-on approach. No study is named, so nothing to misquote.

**Cast:** our dog (v2), the owner, a NEW second dog (another breed, Flow photos on the dog v2 pipeline), the dog walker (Mac cast) holding it. **Sets:** kit street + park, `scale_set(1.35)`, ENCUADRE rules.

## Script (draft, before humanizer)

| # | Beat | Voice | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | claim | Your dog thinks the new dog wants a fight… *(bark)* because of your leash. | Street: the two dogs lunge at each other, leashes taut; hero bark in the gap. |
| 2 | premise | On a walk, two dogs meet face to face… and it goes bad fast. | Owner and walker approach; the dogs are dragged nose to nose; snarl. |
| 3 | dog logic | In dog language, walking straight up and staring is a challenge. | Close on the new dog's locked stare; text "CHALLENGE". |
| 4 | behaviour | Off the leash, they curve in… and sniff each other's rear. A tight leash skips all that. | Park: the dogs circle on a dotted curve and sniff; cut back to the taut leash. |
| 5 | punchline | So give them some slack… and let them be polite. Butt first. | The owner lets the leash go slack; the dogs sniff rears, tails wagging; the owner looks away, awkward. |
