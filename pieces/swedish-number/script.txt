Sweden once gave its phone number to the entire planet. Not a call center. Not a tourist office. Just… Sweden. You dial it from your couch, it rings, and somewhere up north a random person picks up. A farmer. A student. A guy who's literally in the middle of eating meatballs. And you can ask them anything. "Why are you all so tall?" "Do you really eat dinner at five?" "Is it actually dark all winter?" Then the guy on the other end chews, swallows, and says, "Wait. Who gave you this number?" You see, this was the Swedish Number. A Swedish tourism group set it up to celebrate two hundred and fifty years since Sweden became one of the first countries to get rid of censorship. So instead of an ad with actors and a script, they let any Swede who downloaded an app become the voice of the whole country. No training. No rules about what to say. And that's the risky part. One call could get you a poet, the next could get you someone who just woke up. But people loved it. In its first couple of months, it got close to two hundred thousand calls from almost every country on Earth. Which means a lot of the world's first impression of Sweden… came from someone in pajamas who just wanted to finish their meatballs.
