There's a library where you don't borrow books. You borrow people. You walk up to the desk, and the librarian hands you a catalog. But instead of titles like "Cooking for Beginners," it says "Refugee." "Former Prisoner." "Police Officer." "Covered in Tattoos." You pick one, just to be bold. "Former Prisoner, please." The librarian stamps your card and says, "Thirty minutes. Please return him in the same condition." And then an actual man walks over and sits down across from you. Now it's awkward. You've got a hundred questions and you're scared to ask a single one. But that's the whole point. He's the book, and you're allowed to ask him anything. Then the lady at the next table leans over, holding her own borrowed book, a firefighter. "Mine's way more interesting than yours, by the way." You see, this started in Denmark, and it's called the Human Library. The books are real volunteers, usually people others judge from far away, and the rules are simple. You sit down, you ask, they answer, honestly. Because it's really easy to judge a whole group of people… and really hard to judge the one sitting right in front of you. Now it runs in dozens of countries, and its motto says it all. Don't judge a book by its cover… just borrow it.
