# The Entire Ocarina of Time Story, Told on an Ancient Tapestry

Narration: Mad 3 (VoiceStudio), as in zelda-fairy. Paragraph = one voice take. Lines in *[brackets]* are retention beats (not spoken).
Chapter titles ("I · THE BOY WITHOUT A FAIRY") are on-screen cards, held 1.5 s longer than in Odyssey p1/p2, not spoken.
Pace reference: the Odyssey's ~5,100 spoken words came out at ~29 min (~176 wpm with cards and holds). This script: 4,629 spoken words ≈ 26.3 min. Humanizer pass done (no dashes in narration, contrasts and closers cut).

Hook (hookscore 58, WORKABLE, "The Impossible Claim"), chosen from:
- 58 WORKABLE: Every hundred years, one boy is born in the Gerudo desert. You know how he ends. You probably never heard how he began.
- 55 WORKABLE: You know the Hero of Time saved Hyrule. You probably missed that he lost seven years doing it, and when it was over, nobody remembered him.
- 48 WEAK: You know the Hero of Time saved Hyrule. What almost nobody remembers is that when it was over, Hyrule didn't remember him at all.
- 45 WEAK (The Question): Why does the Hero of Time end Ocarina of Time alone, seven years younger, in a world that has never heard of him?
- 41 WEAK: You think Ocarina of Time ends with the hero winning. He wins, then loses seven years, his fairy, and every person who knew his name.
The opening uses the 58 as line one and the 55 as line two, so both tragedies (the king and the boy) are set up in the first 15 s.

## 0 · Cold open

Every hundred years, one boy is born in the Gerudo desert. You know how he ends. You probably never heard how he began.

And the forest boy who stopped him won. Then he lost seven years of his life, and he woke up in a world where nobody remembered a single thing he'd done.

This is the whole story of Ocarina of Time, from the three goddesses who made the world to the night time split in two.

*[BEAT: promise — the tapestry unrolls left to right; we follow it]*

It starts before there were any people at all.

## Prologue · The Three Goddesses

Long ago, before life, before anything had a name, three golden goddesses came down to an empty world.

Din, the goddess of power, shaped the red earth with her strong arms. Nayru, the goddess of wisdom, poured her wisdom over it and gave the world its laws. And Farore, the goddess of courage, made the living things that would follow those laws.

Then the three of them went back to the heavens. Where they left the world, they left something behind: three golden triangles, joined into one. People would call it the Triforce.

Whoever touched it would have their wish granted. Any wish at all.

*[BEAT: open loop — the rule that matters at the end]*

But there was a catch, and it's the most important thing in this whole story. If the person who touched it didn't have a balance of power, wisdom and courage in their heart, the Triforce would break into three. The one who touched it would keep only the piece they believed in most. The other two would go looking for someone else.

To keep it safe, the Triforce was sealed away in a hidden world called the Sacred Realm. And the land the goddesses had made came to be called Hyrule.

## I · The Boy Without a Fairy

Deep in the woods of Hyrule lived the Kokiri. They were children, and they stayed children forever. Each of them had a fairy of their own, a little ball of light that floated over their shoulder and told them what to do.

Each of them except one.

His name was Link. He looked like the others. He wore the same green clothes and lived in the same little tree houses. But he had no fairy, and the other kids never let him forget it. The worst of them was a boy called Mido, who liked to call him "Mr. No Fairy".

His one friend was a girl named Saria. She didn't care about the fairy. She played songs for him on a little wooden ocarina, and she treated him like he belonged.

At night, Link kept having the same dream. A storm over a castle. A girl on a white horse, riding away. And a huge man in black armour on a black horse, turning to look straight at him.

*[BEAT: the forest's guardian]*

The forest had a guardian, the Great Deku Tree. He was ancient, and he was the father of every Kokiri in those woods.

And he was dying.

A man from the desert had come into the forest and put a curse on him. The curse lived inside the tree, a huge spider-like creature eating him from the roots.

So the Deku Tree did something he'd put off for ten years. He sent the boy a fairy. Her name was Navi, and the first thing she ever said to Link was "Hey! Wake up!"

Mido wouldn't let Link see the tree without a sword and a shield. So Link found a little sword in a chest hidden at the edge of the village, saved up forty rupees for a wooden shield, and walked up to the Great Deku Tree with Navi buzzing at his ear.

The tree opened his mouth, and Link climbed inside.

He went down through the roots, through the webs, all the way to the bottom, and he killed the creature that was feeding on the tree. The curse was broken.

He was too late.

*[BEAT: the first death]*

The Deku Tree told Link that the man who cursed him was a wicked man of the desert, and that he was after the Triforce. Then he gave Link a green stone, the Kokiri's Emerald, which the man had wanted badly enough to kill for. He told the boy to go to Hyrule Castle and find the princess of the land.

And then the Great Deku Tree died. His leaves went grey, and his bark went dry.

The other Kokiri thought Link had killed him. Kokiri weren't supposed to leave the forest. They said if they did, they'd die. Link left anyway.

Saria was waiting for him on the bridge out of the woods. She'd known he would go, she said. She'd always known he was different. And she gave him her ocarina, so he wouldn't forget her.

Then Link walked out of the forest for the first time in his life.

## II · The Man from the Desert

To understand what happens next, you have to go far to the west, past the valley, into the desert.

The Gerudo lived there. They were a nation of women. Thieves, mostly, and proud of it. Tall and red-haired, they lived in a fortress cut into the rock, and they rode out into the sand to take what the desert wouldn't give them.

Once every hundred years, a single boy was born among them. By Gerudo law, that boy would be their king.

*[BEAT: the one man]*

The boy of this hundred years was called Ganondorf.

Two witches raised him, twin sisters named Koume and Kotake, together called Twinrova. One of them used fire and the other used ice. They told everyone they were four hundred years old. They taught him magic. They taught him that the desert was too small for him.

The desert was all he had. In the day, the sun burned the sand white, and the wind scorched everything it touched. At night, the cold came through the walls. Whatever time it blew, the wind brought death.

Years later, in another game, a very old Ganondorf would remember it that way. He'd say that the wind over the green fields of Hyrule brought something else. And he'd say that he wanted that wind.

I find that line hard to shake. At the start, all he wanted was what the people on the other side of the mountains already had.

*[BEAT: open loop — what the king really wanted]*

So he went to Hyrule as the King of the Gerudo, and he knelt in front of the King of Hyrule and swore that he was loyal.

He was lying, of course. He'd heard the old stories about a golden relic hidden in a sacred world. And if it was true that the Triforce would give you whatever you wanted, then he wanted it more than anyone alive.

## III · The Princess in the Garden

Link snuck into Hyrule Castle. He climbed the vines, crawled through the drains, and slipped past the guards, and at the very back of the castle he found a little garden. A girl was standing at a window there, watching the throne room.

She was Princess Zelda.

She wasn't surprised to see him. She'd had a dream, she said. Dark clouds over Hyrule, and then a light that came out of the forest holding a green stone, with a fairy flying by its side. She asked him if he had the stone.

He did.

*[BEAT: the man at the window]*

She pointed at the window. Inside the throne room, a big man with red hair was kneeling in front of her father.

The man from the desert, she said. Ganondorf. The man in black from Link's dream.

Zelda was sure he was lying to her father. She'd felt it. Everyone else trusted him, but she'd seen the way he looked when nobody was watching. She was sure he was after the Triforce, and that if he got it, he'd use it to make himself king of everything.

So she had a plan. Ganondorf couldn't reach the Sacred Realm on his own. To open the way, you needed three Spiritual Stones, one from the forest, one from the mountain and one from the water, and the Ocarina of Time, which her family kept safe. If Link brought her the other two stones, she said, they could open the door first, take the Triforce, and use it to stop him.

She was ten years old, and she was trying to save the world. I don't think she could have known what she was really asking.

On his way out, Zelda's guardian Impa taught Link a song, the one the royal family sang to Zelda when she was little. Zelda's Lullaby. It would open a lot of doors.

## IV · The Spiritual Stones

The second stone was up on Death Mountain, the volcano above the village of Kakariko, where the Gorons lived.

The Gorons were huge, round, rock-hard people who ate stone for breakfast, lunch and dinner. And they were starving.

*[BEAT: what Ganondorf did to the Gorons]*

Ganondorf had come up the mountain first. He asked their leader, Darunia, for the Goron's Ruby, their Spiritual Stone. Darunia said no. So Ganondorf sealed up Dodongo's Cavern, the cave where the Gorons got their best rocks, and filled it with monsters.

Darunia was too angry and too proud to talk to anyone, until Link played him Saria's song. Darunia started dancing. A huge rock man, dancing his heart out in his throne room. Then he told Link that if he cleared out the cavern, the Gorons would give him whatever he wanted.

Link went down through the lava pits, and at the bottom he fought King Dodongo, a giant lizard that swallowed everything in its path. Link fed it a bomb.

When he came out, the Gorons cheered for him, and Darunia named him his Sworn Brother. He gave him the Goron's Ruby.

*[BEAT: second stone — one more]*

The last stone belonged to the Zoras, the fish people who lived where the river began, behind a waterfall.

Their king was frantic. His daughter, Princess Ruto, had gone missing. She'd gone to visit Lord Jabu-Jabu, a huge whale-like fish the Zoras worshipped. And now Jabu-Jabu was sick and wouldn't let anyone near.

Link let the giant fish swallow him.

Ruto was inside, and she was rude about being rescued. She'd lost something in there, she said, a stone her mother had given her, and she wasn't leaving without it. So Link carried her through the guts of a fish god while she complained the whole way.

There was a parasite inside Jabu-Jabu too, a big tangle of electric jellyfish. Ganondorf had put it there. Link killed it, and the fish spat both of them out.

The stone Ruto had been looking for was Zora's Sapphire. She gave it to him. Then she told him that, among the Zoras, that stone is only ever given to the person you're going to marry. She'd be waiting for him.

Link didn't really have an answer to that, so he left.

He had all three stones now. He started back for the castle.

## V · The Betrayal

When Link got to the castle gate, it was raining. The drawbridge came down.

A white horse burst out of the gate at full gallop. Impa was riding it, and Zelda was holding on behind her. As they went past, Zelda looked back at Link and threw something into the moat.

A moment later, a black horse came after them. Ganondorf was riding it.

*[BEAT: the dream comes true]*

It was Link's dream, almost exactly.

Ganondorf had finally made his move against the King. Zelda was running for her life. Ganondorf stopped his horse in front of the boy and asked which way they'd gone. Link drew his little sword. Ganondorf laughed at him and knocked him down with one blast of dark magic. Then he rode off into the rain.

Link got up and pulled the thing Zelda had thrown out of the moat. It was the Ocarina of Time, the royal family's treasure.

When he held it, he heard her voice. She'd left him a message. She told him to take the stones to the Temple of Time and play the song she was about to teach him.

The Temple of Time stood in the market town below the castle. Link put the three Spiritual Stones on the altar and played the Song of Time.

The great stone door at the back opened.

*[BEAT: the sword]*

Behind it was a quiet room with no one in it. In the middle of the room was a pedestal, and in the pedestal there was a sword. The Master Sword. The blade of evil's bane.

Link grabbed the hilt and pulled.

Light filled the room. And when Link pulled the sword out, he also opened the way to the Sacred Realm. The Master Sword was the last lock on that door.

That's the moment Ganondorf had been waiting for.

He'd never been chasing Zelda. He'd been following Link. He walked into the temple behind the boy and laughed. He thanked him. The door he couldn't open, a child had opened for him. Then Ganondorf walked into the Sacred Realm, and he put his hand on the Triforce.

## VI · Seven Years

Remember the rule from the very beginning.

When Ganondorf touched the Triforce, it broke apart. His heart wasn't in balance. It was all power. So he kept the Triforce of Power, the piece that belonged to Din. The other two pieces flew away to find the people they belonged to.

The Triforce of Wisdom went to Zelda.

And the Triforce of Courage went to Link.

*[BEAT: the boy is too young]*

Link didn't know any of that yet. When he pulled the Master Sword, he was ten years old, and he was too young to carry it. The sword couldn't let a child be the hero, so it sealed his spirit away to wait until he was old enough.

He slept for seven years.

When he woke up, he was in a strange place, a floating platform of light, with an old man standing over him. The man said his name was Rauru. He'd built the Temple of Time long ago, and he was the Sage of Light.

Rauru told Link that seven years had passed. He told him what Ganondorf had become in that time. With the Triforce of Power, he wasn't the King of the Gerudo any more. He was the King of Evil. He'd taken Hyrule Castle. Monsters walked the fields. And the Sacred Realm itself, where the Triforce had been, had gone dark.

Then Rauru told him what he had to do. There were six more sages. They were alive somewhere in Hyrule, and most of them didn't know who they were yet. Ganondorf had trapped each one in an old temple. If Link woke them, the seven sages together could seal the King of Evil away.

*[BEAT: waking up grown]*

Link opened his eyes back in the Temple of Time. He stood up, and he was tall. His arms were long. He was holding the Master Sword, and it fit his hand.

He was seventeen.

Outside, the market town was in ruins, and the dead shuffled through its empty streets. Above it all, where Hyrule Castle used to be, there was a black fortress floating over a pit of lava.

And standing in the temple, waiting for him, there was someone he'd never met, wrapped in blue and white cloth, with a red eye stitched on the chest. The stranger was called Sheik, one of the last of the Sheikah, Impa's people.

Sheik had been waiting for the Hero of Time to wake up. Sheik knew where the temples were, and knew the songs that would take Link there. And Sheik knew a lot more than they were saying.

## VII · The Temples

Link went home first.

The Kokiri Forest was full of monsters now, and the Kokiri hid inside their houses. None of them recognised him. They'd stayed children, and he'd grown up, which a Kokiri never does.

A small sprout was growing where the Great Deku Tree had died. It told him the truth. Link was a Hylian. When he was a baby, during a terrible war, his mother had run into the forest, wounded, carrying him. She left him with the Deku Tree and died there. The tree had raised him as a Kokiri, so nobody would ever find him.

*[BEAT: Saria]*

Saria was missing. She'd gone to the old Forest Temple deep in the woods, and she'd never come back.

Link went in after her. The temple was full of ghosts, four sisters who'd stolen its flames, and at the top of it he fought a phantom on a horse, a copy of Ganondorf made out of shadow.

When the phantom was gone, Saria appeared to him, standing in the Chamber of Sages. She was the Sage of the Forest. That was why she'd gone. She told him she'd always be his friend, and that she had to stay where she was now, holding the forest together, so he couldn't bring her home.

*[BEAT: Darunia]*

On Death Mountain, the Gorons were gone. Ganondorf had taken them all to the Fire Temple inside the volcano and was going to feed them to a dragon, Volvagia, an ancient fire dragon the Gorons had beaten long ago and brought back to life by the King of Evil.

Darunia had gone in to stop it alone.

Link freed the Gorons one by one from their cells. Then he killed the dragon with a hammer. When it was over, Darunia stood in the Chamber of Sages too, the Sage of Fire. He called Link his Brother one last time, and he didn't come back either.

*[BEAT: Ruto]*

Zora's Domain was frozen solid. The waterfall had turned to ice. King Zora was sealed in a block of red ice on his throne, and almost all of his people had frozen with him.

Ruto had escaped, and she'd gone to free the Water Temple at the bottom of Lake Hylia. She found Link there, all grown up, and she still remembered the engagement.

The Water Temple was a maze of rising and falling water. Link had to drain the lake and fill it up again, over and over. At the bottom he fought a creature made of living water. When it was gone, the lake came back, and Ruto was awake as the Sage of Water.

Even as a sage, she made sure he knew they were still engaged.

## VIII · Sheik

When Link came down to Kakariko, the village was on fire.

The bottom of the old well had cracked open, and something had come out of it, a dark shape that smashed through the houses. Sheik was standing in front of it, and it threw Sheik across the square. Then it went back down into the ground.

It came from the Shadow Temple, under the village graveyard, where the Sheikah had once done things for the royal family that nobody talked about. Impa had gone after it.

*[BEAT: Impa]*

The Shadow Temple was the worst place Link went. It was full of torture rooms, guillotines, invisible walls and things in the dark. At the bottom of it he fought Bongo Bongo, a monster Impa had sealed in the bottom of the well long ago. It was a pair of giant hands drumming on a stretched skin, and one eye.

When he'd beaten it, Impa was waiting for him too. The Sage of Shadow. She told him to take care of the princess.

*[BEAT: the last sage — back to the desert]*

One sage was left, and it was in the desert, in Ganondorf's own home.

Link crossed the valley into the Gerudo lands. He snuck past the guards, crossed the Haunted Wasteland in a sandstorm and found the Spirit Temple, a giant stone figure of a goddess carved into the cliffs.

But the temple wouldn't let him in. The way in was too small for a grown man. So Link put the Master Sword back in its pedestal, went back seven years and came to the desert as a child.

There he met Nabooru. She was the second in command of the Gerudo. And she didn't like her king.

She said she was a thief, and proud of it, but she'd never steal from the weak, and she'd never hurt anyone who couldn't fight back. Ganondorf did all of that. She was planning to steal the Silver Gauntlets from the temple, so Ganondorf could never use them.

Before she could, the Twinrova sisters grabbed her and pulled her into the dark.

Seven years later, when the grown-up Link came back, Nabooru was waiting for him inside the temple, in a suit of iron armour, under the witches' spell. He had to fight her without knowing who it was. When he broke her armour, she woke up. Then the witches dragged her away again.

Link climbed to the top of the goddess statue and fought the two witches who'd raised the King of Evil. They fused into one giant witch with fire in one hand and ice in the other, and he beat them. They floated up into the sky, still bickering about how old they were, and disappeared.

Nabooru was the Sage of Spirit. She'd been the witches' puppet for seven years, and now she'd make up for it.

*[BEAT: the reveal]*

When Link came back to the Temple of Time, Sheik was there.

Sheik told him an old legend of the Sheikah. When the Triforce breaks, Sheik said, the piece of courage goes to the hero, and the piece of wisdom goes to the princess of destiny.

Then Sheik raised a hand, and the Triforce of Wisdom lit up on the back of it.

Sheik was Zelda.

She'd been hiding for seven years. The night Ganondorf attacked the castle, Impa had got her out. She'd disguised herself as a Sheikah so Ganondorf wouldn't find her, because as long as she had the Triforce of Wisdom, he would be looking for it.

She'd been next to Link the whole time, on every road, in every temple. She'd taught him the songs and pointed him at the sages.

She gave him the one weapon that could hurt the King of Evil, the Light Arrows. And then, before she could finish telling him the plan, the floor of the temple glowed, and she was gone. Ganondorf had felt her come out of hiding, and he'd snatched her into a crystal.

His voice echoed through the temple. He told Link that he'd taught Zelda a lesson about hiding. If Link wanted her back, he could come to the castle.

## IX · The Tower

The six sages built a bridge of light across the lava pit, and Link walked across it into Ganon's Castle.

Inside, six magic barriers blocked the tower, one for each temple, and he broke them one by one. Then he climbed the tower.

At the top, Ganondorf was sitting at a great pipe organ, playing to himself. Zelda hung in a pink crystal near the ceiling.

*[BEAT: the three pieces in one room]*

Ganondorf stopped playing and stood up. He held up his hand, and the Triforce of Power glowed on it. The Triforce of Courage glowed on Link's hand. And the Triforce of Wisdom glowed on Zelda's, inside the crystal.

All three pieces, in one room, for the first time since they were split.

Ganondorf said he could feel the three pieces calling to each other. They wanted to be one again, and they'd all come to him.

Then he attacked.

He threw balls of dark lightning, and Link sent them back with the Master Sword. He floated over the broken floor, and Link shot him with the Light Arrows Zelda had given him. At the end, Link drove the sword into his chest.

Ganondorf fell, and the crystal broke, and Zelda was free.

But the tower started to fall apart. Ganondorf had used his last strength to bring the castle down on top of them. Link and Zelda ran down the stairs as the walls fell in. They made it out just before the whole thing collapsed.

For a second it was quiet. It was over.

*[BEAT: false ending]*

Then the rubble moved.

Ganondorf climbed out of what was left of his castle, bleeding and furious. The Triforce of Power burned on his hand. And he changed.

He became Ganon. A beast taller than the castle had been, with the head of a boar, two huge swords and a mane of fire. His first swing knocked the Master Sword out of Link's hand, and it went spinning into the ring of fire around them.

Link fought him without it, as long as he could. Then Zelda used everything she had to hold the monster still, just long enough for Link to grab the sword.

He drove it into Ganon's face.

*[BEAT: the sealing]*

Zelda called the sages. The six of them, and Zelda as the seventh, poured their power into the Sacred Realm. The light pulled at the beast and dragged it off the ground and into the dark.

Ganondorf wasn't dead. He was being sealed away, alone, with the Triforce of Power still on his hand.

As he went, he screamed at them. He cursed Zelda, he cursed the sages, and he cursed Link. He told them that one day the seal would break, and when it did, he would wipe out their descendants.

Then the light closed over him.

That was the end of the man from the desert. He'd been born the one boy in a hundred years, raised by two witches who told him he was meant for more, and he walked out of the desert into a world greener than anything he'd ever had. He took all of it, and he ended up alone in the dark, with the piece of a god he'd wanted so badly, and nothing to use it on.

I don't think you have to feel sorry for him to see how sad that is.

## X · Beyond Time

Afterwards, Link and Zelda stood together up in the clouds.

Zelda apologised to him. She said it was all her fault. She was the one who'd been so sure of her plan. She'd tried to reach the Sacred Realm herself, and all she'd done was open the door for Ganondorf. And she'd dragged a boy from the forest into it.

She'd taken seven years from him.

*[BEAT: the gift]*

So she gave them back. She held up the Ocarina of Time, and she said that it was time for Link to go home, to the time where he belonged, to have back the years he'd lost.

She played. And the Hero of Time disappeared.

He woke up in the Temple of Time. He was ten years old again. The Master Sword was back in its pedestal, and he was small enough to look up at it.

Navi floated out of his hat, hovered over him for a second, and then flew up and out of the temple window.

She didn't say goodbye.

*[BEAT: the garden]*

Link walked out of the temple, through the town, up to the castle and into the little garden, where a girl was standing at a window, watching the throne room.

Zelda turned around. She'd never seen him before.

In that garden, none of it had happened yet. Ganondorf hadn't taken the castle. Saria was in the forest playing her songs. Darunia and Ruto and Impa and Nabooru had no idea they were sages.

And none of them knew Link.

*[BEAT: time splits]*

That's the part people keep coming back to. Saving Hyrule split time in two. Nintendo spelled it out years later, in its official history of Hyrule.

In one timeline, the adult Link was gone. He was sent back and never returned, and Hyrule had no hero the next time Ganon broke free. In the other, Link was a child again, with a story nobody would believe, the only person alive who remembered any of it.

His friends were sages in a future that wasn't going to happen, and his fairy was gone. He'd won the hardest fight in the history of Hyrule for a world that would never know about it.

You'll get to walk that whole road again soon, starting in the forest, on the night he had no fairy.

Thanks for staying for all of it.
