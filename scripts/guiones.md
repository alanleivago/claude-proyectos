# Guiones (narración en inglés, ~1100 caracteres, ~60 s)

## 1. Odysseus
Odysseus and his crew sailed into a cave full of cheese, lambs and goats, and waited for its owner to come home. He was Polyphemus, a giant who ate men, and he was the son of the sea god Poseidon. When he rolled back the boulder and sealed the entrance, the sailors were trapped inside. That night he grabbed two of the men and ate them alive. Odysseus knew they could never move the boulder, so he made a plan. He offered the giant strong wine, and Polyphemus drank until he slumped into sleep. Odysseus sharpened a wooden stake in the fire and drove it into the giant's single eye. When the other giants came shouting for help, Polyphemus roared back that Nobody was killing him, so they left. The crew escaped by clinging beneath the sheep as they walked out to pasture. But as the ship pulled away, Odysseus could not stop himself. He shouted his real name across the water, to prove he had won. Polyphemus heard it. He prayed to his father to make Odysseus suffer. And for ten more years, the sea did exactly what he asked.

## 2. Kratos
Kratos made a promise to Ares, the god of war, and the god kept him. He became the Ghost of Sparta, a man who wore his enemies' blood like armor, fought with the Blades of Chaos, and never feared death. He fought the Hydra and the Medusa, and he survived every one of them. Then the gods tricked him. In his rage, he destroyed his own family, and he lost everything he had left. For years he walked the lands of Greece, hunting the gods who had used him. He climbed Olympus. He tore it down. He killed Zeus, the king of the gods, with his own hands. But the past would not let him go. Years later, in the frozen lands of the north, he raised a son named Atreus and tried to teach him to be better than his father. Every battle he won, against gods and monsters alike, carried him closer to the same question: what does a man owe to the family he destroyed? The northern gods were no kinder than the Greek ones. In the end, the god of war learned the cruelest lesson of all. The blood he spilled had always been his own.

## 3. Rhaenyra
Rhaenyra was named heir to the Iron Throne by her own father, and for a while the realm believed it. Her father, King Viserys, had made his lords swear to support her, but the lords of the realm still did not want a queen. When he died, the crown went to her half-brother Aegon, and the great houses split in two. Her supporters were called the Blacks, and Aegon's were called the Greens. Rhaenyra's sons rode dragons into the war. Her young son Lucerys fled across the sky, trying to escape his cousin Aemond. Aemond chased him down on Vhagar, the largest dragon of all. Lucerys fell, and his dragon fell with him. Grief and rage spread through every castle in Westeros. Dragons began falling from the sky, and their riders died with them. Rhaenyra wanted vengeance, and the houses answered her with fire and blood. She had fought for the throne that was promised to her, and she had lost the one thing she could never replace. Now that throne would cost her everything, and the Dance of the Dragons would burn the realm to the ground.

## 4. Ned
Ned was the most honorable man in the Seven Kingdoms, and honor was the one thing that would destroy him. He had been the best friend of King Robert Baratheon, and when Robert asked him to serve as his Hand, Ned left his home in the North, certain that a good man could fix a corrupt court. There he uncovered a secret that could bring the throne down, and it involved the children of the queen herself. His wife, Catelyn, and his daughters Sansa and Arya waited for him at home, and his bastard son Jon was on the Wall. Ned believed the truth would protect his family, and he trusted the people around him. He was wrong. When Robert died, he was arrested for treason, and the men he had trusted turned their backs on him. On the steps of the Great Sept of Baelor, before the people of King's Landing, a boy king gave the order. The crowd went silent as the blade rose. His daughters were there to see it. The North lost its lord that day. The kingdom lost its conscience. And the war that followed had only just begun.

## 5. Baldr
Baldr was the most beloved of the gods. He had dreams of his own death, and they frightened the other gods, so his mother, Frigg, made every living thing swear never to harm him. Fire, water, iron, stone, beast and plant all swore. She overlooked one small plant: the mistletoe. Loki noticed. He carved a dart from it and guided the blind god Höðr to throw it. Baldr fell dead, and the gods wept in the halls of Asgard. For years the gods had thrown weapons at him for sport, and none of them could draw blood. Hermóðr, another son of Odin, rode for nine nights through deep dark valleys on Sleipnir, the eight-legged horse, to the land of the dead. There he begged Hel to release him. She agreed on one condition: everything in the world must weep for Baldr. Everything did, except one giantess, who was Loki in disguise. So Baldr stayed in the realm of the dead. Loki was bound in a cave, with venom dripping onto his face, until the end of the world, and Odin's son Váli grew up to avenge his brother. No oath can keep out the one small thing that was forgotten.

## 6. Prometheus
Prometheus was a Titan, and he loved humanity more than the gods did. His name meant forethought, and he used it to see what the humans needed. They lived in the dark, with no fire, no warmth, and no tools to build a future. So he stole fire from Olympus and hid it in a fennel stalk. Zeus was furious. He ordered the smith god Hephaestus to chain Prometheus to a rock in the Caucasus, where the wind and the cold never stopped, and the chains were made of iron. Then he sent an eagle to tear at his liver every day. The liver grew back every night, so the punishment never ended. Zeus also sent Pandora, and her jar of troubles, to punish mankind for what Prometheus had given it. Hesiod tells the story in the oldest surviving Greek poems, and the ancient writers agreed on the core of it. Prometheus did not beg, and he did not repent. He endured, year after year, in silence. He had given humanity the gift that changed everything, and for that gift he was torn apart each day and made whole each night, only to be torn apart again.

## 7. Harry
Harry had spent his whole life being hunted. He grew up in a cupboard under the stairs, and he had already lost Sirius, Dumbledore and Remus Lupin. Now he walked into the Forbidden Forest to die. Voldemort had given the castle one hour to surrender him. Harry knew that a piece of Voldemort's soul lived inside him, a Horcrux, and that it could only be destroyed if he died. Snape's memories had shown him the truth, and Dumbledore's plan had prepared him for it. He carried his mentor's words in his mind as he walked. So he had to die, so that the others could live. Before he left, he told his friend Neville to destroy Nagini, the last Horcrux. In the forest, he used the Resurrection Stone and saw his parents and his godfather waiting for him. Voldemort had murdered his parents when he was a baby, and now the Dark Lord raised his wand. Then came the curse, and Harry fell. The whole castle believed he was dead. But in the place between life and death, he was offered a choice nobody else would ever get: walk away, or go back and fight. He chose to fight.
